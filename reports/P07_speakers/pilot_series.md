# P07 — Four-session pilot

**as_of_date: 2026-11-15 · Proposed programme, not scheduled or authorised · Sources accessed 2026-11-15**

## Question and place in the programme

**Who makes an economy governable—and who bears the costs?** Move from contested jurisdiction to measurement, financing and political mobilisation. Every session asks students to distinguish an actor's categories from a historian's inference.

Seek four agreed future slots over twelve weeks; **weeks below are relative, not calendar dates**. The current [Histoires de la vie économique programme](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/histoires-de-la-vie-economique/) (P07-S007) and [La matière du politique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/la-matiere-du-politique/) (P07-S033) already publish 2026–27 dates. Integration may mean replacing agreed slots in a later cycle or reducing to one contribution. Do not overlay a new spring 2027 timetable.

All speakers, respondents and formats are proposals. Pairings are inferred from documented work, except the separately noted Calafat–Trivellato coauthorship. Public institutional profile links in `candidates.csv` are the contact routes for later authorised outreach.

## Sessions and preparation

### P07-SES01 · Week 4 · Who governs a wreck?

**Proposed contributor:** Guillaume Calafat (P07-C009). **Respondent:** David Todd, whose work on informal empire and Mediterranean legal sources provides a local intellectual connection (P07-S016–017). Format: 75-minute case discussion; possible Paris in-person session, location and costs unverified. Calafat has prior CHEP-supported appearances; the 2024 article provides the proposed new-work basis.

Read the accessible [IAS abstract of The Shipwreck of the Turks](https://albert.ias.edu/entities/publication/6c55edbd-b125-4643-bc4b-f7592c1d302d) (Calafat and Francesca Trivellato, 2024; P07-S208). **The abstract supports orientation, not a detailed factual reconstruction.** A convenor-approved excerpt or permitted source extract must be secured before an invited paper workshop; otherwise run the source-design variant below.

Agenda: 5 minutes framing; 15 contributor case; 10 respondent; 15 small-group exercise; 25 discussion; 5 synthesis. Students separate what the abstract establishes from the legal records they would need to test claims about authority. Output: one shared “claim / needed source / uncertainty” table. This introduces categories whose costs the next session tries to measure. Do not invent the contents of archival documents.

### P07-SES02 · Week 7 · When a budget leaves out labour

**Proposed contributor:** Marlous van Waijenburg (P07-C003). **Respondent:** Claire Lemercier, on comparative historical measurement (P07-S012–013). Format: 75-minute paper-and-methods discussion, remote option. This shifts from legal authority to the fiscal value of compelled work.

Pre-read the open author essay [Financing the African Colonial State: Fiscal Capacity Building and Forced Labor](https://aehnetwork.org/financing-the-african-colonial-state-fiscal-capacity-building-and-forced-labor/) (21 October 2018; P07-S107), alongside the [journal abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/abs/financing-the-african-colonial-state-the-revenue-imperative-and-forced-labor/9514245BA11D58B053FBB83E4EADF565) (P07-S106). The newer [project abstract](https://www.economics.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5991/files/2026-03/Financing%20the%20African%20Colonial%20State%20Fiscal%20Capacity%20and%20For.pdf) (P07-S108; document date unknown) is optional orientation, not a published book.

Agenda: 5 framing; 15 author; 10 methods response; 15 paired sensitivity exercise; 25 discussion; 5 synthesis. Use a **fictional teaching example**: 100 workers × 10 days × 1 currency unit/day = 1,000 units. Change the assumed wage to 0.5 and the estimate to 500; discuss why neither is a historical observation. Ask how missing people, days and wages affect a lower bound. Output: assumptions and exclusions sheet. Do not claim the inaccessible replication package was checked.

### P07-SES03 · Week 10 · Who governs an indebted city?

**Proposed contributor:** Destin Jenkins (P07-C001). **Respondent:** Nicolas Delalande, on public debt and obligations (P07-S015). Format: 75-minute book conversation, with remote speaker if mutually agreed. The sequence moves from raising resources to the power exercised by their providers.

Read the openly accessible introduction and “Management” chapter **abstracts**, not the full chapters, on [BiblioVault’s The Bonds of Inequality page](https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.landing.epl?ISBN=9780226721682) (2021; P07-S102). An optional permitted chapter requires library or author access. Students draw the actors connecting residents, municipal government, lenders and ratings, then identify which arrows the abstract supports and which require research.

Agenda: 5 framing; 20 author conversation; 10 respondent; 10 student maps; 25 discussion; 5 synthesis. Output: one annotated institutional map and one alternative explanation. Compare financial categories with the omissions identified in session 2 without treating San Francisco and colonial Africa as interchangeable cases.

### P07-SES04 · Week 12 · When cheap energy becomes a political claim

**Proposed contributor:** Elizabeth Chatterjee (P07-C014). **Respondent:** Giacomo Parrinello, on infrastructure, environmental change and state/capital relations (P07-S018). Format: 75-minute comparative paper discussion, remote option. Finish by asking how people politically contest the conditions of economic life.

Pre-read the abstract and opening of [Towards an Energetics of Class: Comparing Energy Protests in India and the United States](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/towards-an-energetics-of-class-comparing-energy-protests-in-india-and-the-united-states/F6ADAA8A37E7C839615D7C4E4F67BCA5) (2024, DOI 10.1017/S0010417524000069; P07-S302; open access). The research assessment inspected the opening and conclusion as well as the abstract. A convenor may assign additional sections after review.

Agenda: 5 framing; 15 author; 10 response; 15 comparative grid; 20 discussion; 10 series synthesis. Students compare ownership, energy dependence, state claims and protest tactics, then name a limit of the comparison. Output: a four-session matrix of authority, measurement, distribution and contestation.

**Duplication gate:** the [host seminar archive](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/la-matiere-du-politique/) records Chatterjee on related research in April 2022. Proceed only if convenors want a return to the published argument and a comparative methods exercise. Otherwise consider Barca on unpaid work and environmental history (P07-C015), with a lawful substantive excerpt required: the [Forces of Reproduction description](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/forces-of-reproduction/BE9B0DBDC89593F3284FE3F51D3B0418) supports only a short orientation.

## Student participation and access

Offer a short English/French glossary and allow questions in either language if the convenor can support this. The proposed readings are primarily English; translation ability and any additional accessibility costs need confirming. Give students two roles per session—source critic and comparison critic—without requiring personal research disclosures. Invite a voluntary 150-word research connection after each session; circulate only with the contributor's permission. Do not reproduce full books, covers or article figures. Public links and original activity sheets suffice.

## Lower-resource local/online version

Use the same four questions as **60-minute text discussions led by an academic convenor**, with no invited author implied. Required texts: Calafat abstract; van Waijenburg author essay; Jenkins abstracts; Chatterjee open article opening. For session 1, ask what sources would be needed rather than reconstructing an unread article. Local students may join in a room; remote access depends on a supported platform. Dermineur's [open author essay](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/banks-historical-lessons-rethinking-credit) is a possible substitute if a more substantial open reading is preferred.

Estimated student work: 6 setup + 4 × 4 session hours + 4 coordination + 2 evaluation = **28 hours/12 weeks (2.33/week)**. Academic time: about 2 preparation/selection + 4 × 2 preparation/hosting + 1 review = **11 hours**, all additional. Cash can be **€0 incremental only conditional on existing room/platform, accessible readings, and agreed staff time**; this is not a claim that the activity is costless. No external contributor is expected to work without agreed terms.

Continue only after an academic owner and suitable slots are identified. After two sessions, adapt or stop if discussion cannot be sustained, if participants find no usable connection, or if workload exceeds the scenario despite rescheduling. After four, retain only the activity sheets and aggregate feedback needed for a decision. Any continued register upkeep is estimated at one student hour/month, outside this pilot total.
