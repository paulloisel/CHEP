## Three recommended shortlist briefs

### Elise Dermineur Reuterswärd — credit as a social relation

**Research contribution (two sentences).** *Before Banks* reconstructs borrowing and lending in rural Alsace, including forms of credit poorly represented by formal banking records. In an accessible author essay, Dermineur uses probate-derived network evidence and discusses repayment flexibility and the social obligations surrounding lending; this supports asking how inclusion depended on local standing, without treating her normative comparison with modern finance as settled fact.

**Question and format.** When does flexible credit protect borrowers, and when does reputation exclude outsiders? A 75-minute book-and-methods conversation could have students identify a network's missing borrowers and distinguish the absence of a formal contract from the absence of a binding obligation. Intended audience: Master's/doctoral researchers in economic and social history, gender, law and quantitative methods. Possible respondent: Claire Lemercier, subject to the parent report's verification of her methodological scholarship; Jonathan Levy is an alternative. Both are proposed matches, not commitments.

**Evidence read.** Publisher synopsis and full author-written [Before banks: Historical lessons for rethinking credit](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/banks-historical-lessons-rethinking-credit) (CEPR VoxEU, 20 April 2025; P07-S203). [Stockholm institutional profile](https://www.su.se/profiles/e/elde6129) (P07-S201) identifies the current title as Universitetslektor, docent; the English author/publisher wording is Associate Professor. The [book](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/before-banks/822A8C3F63F39643F0169EE675442FB6) (P07-S202) has January 2025 print and June 2025 digital dates, not two separate works. Full book was not read.

**Accessible activity.** Use the VoxEU figure caption about Florimont probate inventories, 1780–85. Ask students to list what a creditor–debtor network can establish, what probate selection may conceal, and what evidence would distinguish solidarity from coercion. Share a link; do not reproduce the image without rights review.

**Practical unknowns.** Actual departure location, availability, preferred language, advance material, travel/honorarium and funding. Stockholm affiliation does not establish travel plans.

### Hannah Farber — private contracts and public power

**Research contribution (two sentences).** *Underwriters of the United States* connects marine insurance to state formation and early American commercial governance. Farber's accessible case essay examines litigation over a wartime voyage, demonstrating how insurance clauses and multiple imperial authorities complicate a simple opposition between private merchants and the state.

**Question and format.** Can an insurance contract govern an empire? A source workshop could give one student group the merchant's position, another the insurer's, and another the jurisdictional problem before comparing their arguments. Intended audience: historians of capitalism, early America, Atlantic commerce, law and risk. Proposed respondents: Jonathan Levy or Paris-based Guillaume Calafat; intellectual fit is inferred.

**Evidence read.** Full author essay [Insurance For (and Against) the Empire](https://commonplace.online/article/insurance-for-and-against-the-empire/) (*Commonplace*, April 2022; P07-S206), publisher synopsis of [Underwriters](https://uncpress.org/9781469699073/underwriters-of-the-united-states/) (P07-S205), and [Columbia profile](https://history.columbia.edu/person/farber-hannah/) (updated 29 October 2026; P07-S204). This is a 2021 book with an August 2026 paperback edition. The profile-linked repository prologue/introduction returned an anti-bot challenge; it was not read.

**Accessible activity.** Read the essay's case narrative through the presentation of the parties' arguments, then identify which contractual, colonial and imperial authorities might decide the dispute. Students should distinguish documented ruling from Farber's explicitly inferential account of its rationale. The essay has links to legal records, but these original records have not been independently inspected in this subtask.

**Practical unknowns.** The profile explicitly records leave in Fall 2026 and Spring 2027. Record this as a scheduling flag, not as evidence of unavailability or a particular travel location. Current title is **Associate Professor**, taking precedence over an old assistant-professor article bio and a generic publisher “professor” description.

### Guillaume Calafat — maritime jurisdiction through a single event

**Research contribution (two sentences).** The 2024 article with Francesca Trivellato uses a 1716 shipwreck involving Muslim pilgrims to investigate overlapping claims of sovereignty and the place of non-Christian polities in international law. Its abstract argues that contested coastal authority and continuing practices of accommodation complicate accounts of a settled legal order after Utrecht.

**Question and format.** Who governs a wreck: captain, coast, flag or sovereign? A paper discussion should ask students to diagram jurisdictional claims and identify whose voices are missing from a legal-diplomatic archive. Intended audience: early modern, Mediterranean, empire, legal and economic history. M'hamed Oualdi is an inferred nearby respondent candidate based on transimperial Maghreb research, pending parent source crosswalk. Francesca Trivellato is a **documented coauthor** and could only be presented as a hypothetical co-presenter/discussant, not an independent respondent.

**Evidence read.** Current [Paris 1 profile](https://www.pantheonsorbonne.fr/page-perso/guillaume.calafat@) (P07-S207) and full [IAS repository abstract](https://albert.ias.edu/entities/publication/6c55edbd-b125-4643-bc4b-f7592c1d302d) (P07-S208). Full article not read; HAL presented an anti-bot challenge. The profile lists a 2026 maritime-nationality article as a possible follow-up lead, but no argument from that article is claimed here.

**Accessible activity.** Read the IAS abstract before the session and construct an initial jurisdiction map; label it a hypothesis map. A full paper exercise would depend on obtaining legitimate access and the convenor choosing an extract.

**Repeat-speaker and practical flags.** Parent research documents a 2023 CHEP-associated appearance with an unresolved 10/17 October date discrepancy. A return should explicitly advance the 2024 shipwreck study rather than duplicate the earlier legal-pluralism talk. Paris affiliation suggests potentially lower travel burden, not zero cost or availability.
