# P06 communications playbook

**as_of_date: 2026-11-15 · Proposed workflow; not implemented**

This playbook supports a three-issue experiment within the existing communications arrangement. It does not authorise distribution. Source titles, dates and retrieval details are in `sources.csv`; the editorial examples are in `pilot_newsletter.md`.

## 1. Purpose and boundaries

Help readers discover scholarship, enter an appropriate discussion and act on a relevant opportunity. Prefer one useful action over a catalogue of loosely related announcements. CHEP's public home advertises an events list (P06-S001); CHSP already describes a newsletter workflow (P06-S019). Confirm their relationship before implementation. If the existing list is intended only for event alerts, propose a digest block in the host newsletter or seek an appropriate institutional arrangement; do not assume subscribers requested a different service.

Use these attribution labels: **CHEP-organised**, **CHEP-supported**, **work featured by CHEP**, **research by a documented current/former affiliate**, and **wider-network/host news**. Use only the label supported by the record. Publication, institutional affiliation and funding are distinct claims.

## 2. Reusable issue template

```text
DRAFT until editorial approval
Issue ID / date / principal language / evidence cutoff
English subject and French subject
Preview: one sentence with the reader's useful action
English opening + French opening (60–80 words each)
Research: two short linked notices, with year and attribution
Coming next: up to two verified events, access and local time stated
Opportunity: one verified call with degree stage and separate deadlines
Community: one contribution or explicitly dated retrospective
Participate: one working institutional route and one modest invitation
Existing administrator-supplied sender/preferences/unsubscribe footer
Archive URL, once created by authorised web editor
```

Target 600–900 words; optional sections can disappear. If there is no verified upcoming event or call, say that this issue is a research digest and use a shorter format. Never invent a “save the date”, deadline, member quotation or planned talk to fill the template. Internal source notes stay outside reader copy.

## 3. Selection rubric

First apply mandatory gates: a public source or contribution approved for publication; an evidenced institutional relationship; accurate dates; a functioning destination or honest contact fallback; and no unresolved factual conflict central to the item. An unverified candidate stays in the queue. Proposed issues and editorial interpretations use status `inferred`; source-supported facts use `verified`; unresolved disagreements use `conflicting`.

For candidates passing the gates, assess three dimensions from 0 to 2: usefulness to a defined reader, contribution to scholarly exchange, and timeliness or durable reading value. Scores guide discussion, not claims about research quality. Prefer items scoring at least 4/6, unless a service notice is essential. Give attention to students and earlier-career researchers, methods and languages; avoid choosing solely on seniority or publisher prestige. Do not infer demographics from names.

Keep a minimal item register: P06 item ID, title, type, source URL/title, publication date, event/deadline date, access date, affiliation/support evidence, proposed section, evidence status, expiry/recheck date, reviewer function and rights note. A future event is **announced**, not proven held. Keep editions and original works connected. A book title alone permits a bibliographic notice, not a summary of its argument.

## 4. Voluntary submission form — draft only

Introduce it as: “Optional: suggest a research item or opportunity for editorial consideration. Submission does not guarantee inclusion or subscribe you to a mailing list. Please send public links, not confidential material.”

1. What would you like readers to discover? Give a title and item type.
2. In up to 80 words, why might it matter to researchers or students?
3. What is the public source, DOI, repository or official event/call URL?
4. What is the connection to CHEP? Give the relevant role/date or support evidence.
5. Is there a publication date, event date or deadline? Include time zone if stated, separate reference deadline and any eligibility/access restrictions.
6. What action can a reader take, and where are the current instructions?
7. What language is the item in, and is there an existing approved translation?
8. May the supplied summary and your chosen professional attribution be edited and published? If a factual question arises, what professional contact route may the editor use?

Do not ask for student numbers, subscriber details, demographic data or unpublished manuscripts. Images are optional and omitted by default; any proposed image needs rights and credit information. Do not create or distribute this form in the present task. If adopted, use the host's approved mechanism; keep editorial contacts out of public archives and apply the host's retention policy.

## 5. Workflow, roles and time budget

Roles are suggested functions, not assigned commitments. The public [organisation page](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html) distinguishes coordination, cross-centre coordination and web architecture; those titles alone do not establish responsibility for newsletter approval.

| Step | Timing for each issue | Student hours | Suggested support function |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Collect public candidates and voluntary contributions | D−21 to D−14 | 1.00 | Researchers may contribute; no contribution promised |
| Verify sources, dates, role/support, access and rights | D−14 to D−10 | 1.25 | Academic reviewer resolves substantive questions |
| Select and edit concise copy | D−10 to D−7 | 1.25 | Student editor |
| Prepare bilingual opening and action synopsis | D−7 to D−5 | 0.75 | Bilingual reviewer checks meaning |
| Coordinate review and incorporate one revision | D−5 to D−2 | 0.75 | Academic/communications reviewer |
| Final dates/link check and administrator handoff | D−1 to D | 1.00 | Existing list administrator performs authorised send |
| Archive copy and correction log | D to D+2 | 0.50 | Existing web editor provides destination |
| **Per-issue total** | | **6.50** | **Additional staff: 0.75 h review/language + 0.25 h distribution** |

Separately budget 0.25 student hour/week for checking expiring notices and recording voluntary responses. Eight setup hours cover workflow/register (5), template/language rules (2) and archive/evaluation design (1). A two-hour final review completes the experiment. Initial staff support: estimated 1.5 hours across the communications and web functions. These assumptions require confirmation.

Let L be the first approved release, currently unassigned. The 18-week planning window runs from L−3 weeks to L+15 weeks; issues fall at L, L+6 weeks and L+12 weeks, with final review at L+14 weeks. For each cycle, place 2 hours of production in each of the three weeks preceding release, then 0.5 hour archiving in release week, plus weekly checking. Spread setup across the three weeks preceding L: about 2.67 hours/week alongside 2 production hours and 0.25 monitoring hour, below the five-hour cap. Other tasks must fit the remaining time in those setup weeks. The 18-week experiment totals 34 student hours; its average is not permission to exceed five hours in a deadline week. If another student task peaks, cut an optional section or move the issue. Do not use unreviewed copy to meet a cadence.

Cash scenario: €0 incremental expenditure if existing email, document and web tools are available. No subscription is purchased. A paid platform, external translation or new development requires a separately scoped cost decision; amounts are unknown. Full bilingual duplication is an optional estimated 2–3 extra student hours per issue, plus staff review. The default translates the opening and essential action facts while linking original sources.

## 6. Approval and publication checklist

The proposed editor prepares a complete reviewable issue; institutional functions then confirm accuracy and publication suitability. This run stops at drafts because the research brief explicitly requires it, not because routine research needs approval.

- Verify the next event/call directly again within 24 hours of proposed circulation. If the authoritative page cannot be checked, remove the actionable claim or seek organiser verification through the authorised workflow.
- Treat the pilot's 17 November notice as expired after that date; do not carry it into a later issue. Recheck December notices too. A deadline without an hour remains without an hour.
- Compare French and English dates and eligibility wording. Preserve source titles; do not translate a title as though it were an official edition.
- Have the reviewer resolve unclear Paris/network attribution. No copied historical title becomes a present role.
- Use descriptive links, semantic headings and readable plain text. No essential information should be an image. Add alt text and rights credits only if approved images are later used.
- Test the web and email versions, including mobile display, when an actual template exists. Confirm registration and preferences links without subscribing or registering on a reader's behalf.
- Administrator checks sender identity, recipient expectations and existing preferences/unsubscribe controls. Do not export the list to the student or build a second list.
- Store the approved text with issue date and stable URL in the existing archive, or a simple archive index if the web function agrees. Keep private contributions and contacts separate.
- Correct public errors at the canonical page and archive, recording correction date. Use an additional correction email only if the institutional editor judges the error materially affects action.

## 7. Reuse from one verified record

For the pilot's November session (P06-S023–S024), keep one record containing organiser, CHEP series support, date, access statement and canonical event URL. The digest gets a short notice; a shared social post gets topic/date plus the event link; the archive preserves the issue's historical notice. All formats point back to the event page rather than reproducing changing registration mechanics. No post is sent here.

For a publication (P06-S025–S027), retain bibliographic metadata and one checked description. The digest can pair it with an editorial discussion question; a web bibliography keeps the citation; social copy can signpost the publisher. None becomes evidence of CHEP funding or author participation in a forthcoming event.

For a call, preserve stage restrictions and both dates even in a short notice. Link to the official terms and expire the action invitation after the deadline. Reuse a record, not an unchecked previous email.

## 8. Evaluate after three issues

At D+14 for each issue, record aggregate voluntary feedback and time spent. A proposed feedback sentence, for later approval, is: “Did something here help you find a useful reading or conversation? A brief reply is welcome; no response is needed.” Do not add this to a live list until a monitored reply route is agreed.

| Measure | Definition | Baseline / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Research interest | Voluntary reports of a research question, reading exchange or contact prompted by an item | `not_found_publicly`; count reported actions, not presumed influence |
| Event discovery | Optional attendee feedback saying an issue helped them find the event | No compulsory sign-in linkage; distinguish response count from attendance |
| Repeat contribution | Contributors appearing in at least two pilot issues | Denominator is actual contributors, not all affiliates |
| Existing readership | Delivery or link metrics already lawfully available to the authorised operator | Availability unknown; opens alone are not intellectual engagement |
| Editorial sustainability | Logged student/staff hours, correction needs and approval delay | Compare with budget; record missing data rather than zero |

At 14 weeks after launch, provisionally continue if at least three useful actions are voluntarily reported across at least two issues, content and review remain dependable, and issue production stays within 6.5 student hours on average. This small threshold is a design choice, not a statistical demonstration of impact. If there are no responses, distinguish weak evidence from proven absence of benefit; offer a brief optional check before deciding. If overlap dominates, consolidate into a host-newsletter block. If reviewer availability or accuracy fails, stop the proposed format. Do not promise audience growth.
