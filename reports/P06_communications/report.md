# P06 — A useful CHEP research digest

**as_of_date: 2026-11-15 · Research and proposals only · No distribution or publication**

## Decision brief

**What we learned.** CHEP already advertises an events mailing list and a joint Cambridge–Harvard–Paris social account. Its host, the Centre d'histoire de Sciences Po (CHSP), also has a newsletter. The opportunity is to assemble useful research and participation information within that existing arrangement. Public evidence does not establish demand for another subscription. No CHEP-specific public issue archive was located in the checked corpus; the mailing-list pages could not be retrieved, so this is a bounded finding, not proof that no newsletter exists. See the [CHEP home](https://www.histecon.fr/en/), [joint Bluesky profile](https://bsky.app/profile/historyecon.bsky.social) and [CHSP communications guidance, printed page 9](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/files/Guidedoctorant2025-2026-15sept.pdf).

**What CHEP could do.** Try three compact issues, six weeks apart, using its established communications infrastructure if the responsible staff confirm that the format fits. The [pilot newsletter](pilot_newsletter.md) demonstrates the editorial proposition: research worth discussing, a usable route into a CHEP-supported seminar, and accurate information on a current call. Content need and reader preferences remain untested.

**What the student can deliver next.** The completed draft, channel audit and workflow are ready for a short editorial discussion. Budget **8 setup hours**, **6.5 hours per issue**, **0.25 hour weekly checking**, and **2 final-review hours**: **34 student hours over a proposed 18-week window**, or about **1.9 hours/week**. ## Findings: channels, content and audiences

### A communication system already exists

The [channel audit](channel_audit.csv) distinguishes Paris channels, host channels and shared-network channels. CHEP's English home links events, books, announcements, contact and mailing-list information. The announcements archive is a news collection, not an identified sequence of newsletter issues. The [contact page](https://histecon.fr/en/contacts.html) supplies a public institutional route, useful when an embedded registration destination cannot be inspected. Distribution frequency, subscription management, existing newsletter content and list size remain unknown.

Sciences Po's [English CHEP page](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/research/centre-for-history-and-economics-in-paris-chep/) includes a “Newsletter” link leading to a Google form. Its scope could not be read, so it cannot be labelled a CHEP-only signup. The dated CHSP guide independently establishes a host newsletter and an existing website-to-newsletter workflow. This makes coordination with the host communications function an immediate dependency, rather than an optional dissemination extra.

The [Harvard homepage](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/) and [news archive](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/news.html) provide network context, but their events and researchers must retain their institutional attribution. Cambridge's home and historical Paris page failed retrieval. No conclusion about Cambridge's present mailing practice follows from that failure. The [CHSP homepage](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/) also links its own Bluesky and LinkedIn accounts; these belong to the host centre, distinct from the joint centres' account.

Retrieved English and French CHEP home copies show different announcements. However, the web tool identifies the French copy as substantially older. This supports a proposed human check of language parity, not a finding that the live French website is currently neglected. Similarly, an older search rendering named David Todd as coordinator, while opened organisation and host pages name Jonathan Levy. The inspected sources take precedence for present wording; historical notices retain historical titles. The [coverage note](coverage.md) records these distinctions.

### The pilot has timely service content without manufactured novelty

The [Histoires de la vie économique programme](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/histoires-de-la-vie-economique/) explicitly credits CHEP support and welcomes students and researchers from inside and outside Sciences Po. Its two late-2026 sessions supply genuine participation opportunities. The organiser remains CHSP; the support statement does not establish a spending amount or sole CHEP organisation. The November event is imminent at the research cutoff, making revalidation at actual release essential.

The [2027 grant call](https://histecon.fr/en/research_grants.html) provides useful, bounded information for eligible doctoral students. It should carry a degree-stage label in every reuse. The draft pairs it with the [dated 2026 recipients announcement](https://www.sciencespo.fr/ecole-recherche/fr/actualites/quatre-jeunes-chercheurs-histoire-laureats-bourse-chep-2026/), clearly identified as a look back. This makes junior researchers visible without inventing profiles, testimonials or evidence of grant impact.

The research cards use publisher descriptions for [*The Real Economy*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691252551/the-real-economy) and [*Aliénés*](https://www.cnrseditions.fr/catalogue/histoire/alienes/), both featured in [CHEP's publications selection](https://histecon.fr/en/publications.html). Their original years remain visible. Editorial questions invite discussion without asserting that either author has agreed to present. These are accessible introductions based on descriptions, not reviews based on full-text reading. Images and book-cover reproductions are unnecessary for this prototype.

## Sustainable production and decisions

Choose **six-weekly** publication as a workload experiment. It gives enough time for contributions and approval, and reduces pressure to inflate old material into news. A monthly edition would require approximately 6.5 production hours plus about one hour of monitoring each month; a full second-language version adds an estimated 2–3 hours per issue plus reviewer time. A quarterly digest is the fallback if content or support is thin. These are planning estimates, not prices or observed staff performance.

Use English for the principal pilot, French and English subject lines/openings, and preserve original titles. All critical action facts—eligibility, date, venue, access rule—must remain understandable in the brief French synopsis or original linked French source. A bilingual reviewer checks meaning. Do not promise two fully translated editions until demand and editing capacity justify them. Alternate principal language only after a preference check, rather than assuming the community has one working language.

The [playbook](communications_playbook.md) defines selection, voluntary submissions, approval, correction and reuse. Academic review and a language check require an estimated **0.75 staff hour per issue**; distribution another **0.25 hour**. Initial staff agreement/technical setup is estimated at **1.5 hours**. Staff availability and actual infrastructure must be confirmed; unpaid labour is not a cash cost of zero in any broader economic sense.

The [three-issue calendar](editorial_calendar.csv) uses dates relative to launch because no launch is authorised. The supplied November prototype is a dated example, not a promise that its live content will survive a later launch. Use a single item record to feed the digest, event-page update and short social signpost, preserving canonical links and eligibility labels. An archive should reuse approved text rather than create a second publication to maintain.

Evaluate useful exchanges, not asserted reach. The student can record aggregate voluntary reports of research enquiries or attendance influenced by an issue; repeat contributors; and minutes actually spent. Existing readership data may help if already available to the authorised administrator, but no baseline, new tracking pixel or growth promise is assumed. At 14 weeks after launch, continue only if there is evidence of use, dependable review and production within the time cap. Adapt to a host-newsletter block if editing duplicates an existing service. Pause the proposed digest if reliable review or substantive content is missing.

## Methods and limits

Only P06 was executed. `Summary.md` contained the four starting URLs and no further brief. Research followed public navigation, French/English variants, archive links and targeted searches. No other reports were required. All substantive sources and failed retrievals used for this assessment are recorded in [sources.csv](sources.csv); [coverage.md](coverage.md) defines the bounded corpus and remaining checks.

The run uses 15 November 2026 in the user's time zone as its access date. Tool crawl ages are retrieval metadata, not publication dates or archive-snapshot dates. Sources were paraphrased; no private correspondence, subscriber records or interviews were accessed. PDF text was inspected, with visual retrieval limits recorded. Unknown demand, attendance, mailing performance and internal responsibilities remain unknown. The site's 2019 collaboration and 2022 establishment are compatible milestones; a fifth anniversary of establishment would be in 2027, but this pilot makes no anniversary claim or institutional commitment.
