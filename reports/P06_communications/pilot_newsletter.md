## From the bookshelf

**What do we mean by “the economy”?** Jonathan Levy's *The Real Economy: History and Theory* (Princeton University Press, 2025) revisits competing definitions and proposes an account open to historical investigation. The publisher's overview connects Veblen and Keynes to questions about uncertainty, money, demand and capitalism. For a reading conversation, consider this question: how does the definition of an economy change the evidence historians seek? [Explore the book and publisher's overview](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691252551/the-real-economy).

**Following lives through institutions.** In *Aliénés : Une histoire sociale de la folie au XIXe siècle* (CNRS Éditions, 2024), Anatole Le Bras follows patients' trajectories and their changing relationships with relatives, neighbours and public authorities. The publisher's description directs attention beyond the asylum to confinement, social hierarchies and life after institutionalisation. A question to carry into discussion: what becomes visible when an institution is studied through the people who encounter it? [Read the presentation of *Aliénés*](https://www.cnrseditions.fr/catalogue/histoire/alienes/).

Both titles appear in [CHEP's selection of publications by participants](https://histecon.fr/en/publications.html). These are reading suggestions from the existing bookshelf, rather than announcements of November releases.

## Research support: the 2027 CHEP call

Sciences Po PhD students in their **first, second or third year**, from any research centre, may apply for CHEP's 2027 research grants. The call provides access to **up to €1,000 in eligible research expenses during 2027**. Applications close **11 December 2026**; references are due **15 December 2026**. Materials may be in French or English. This call is for doctoral students; it does not establish Master's eligibility. No closing hour is stated. [Read the full call, required documents and application instructions](https://histecon.fr/en/research_grants.html).

**En bref — bourses 2027 :** doctorant·es de Sciences Po en première, deuxième ou troisième année ; jusqu’à 1 000 € de frais de recherche. Candidatures avant le 11 décembre 2026 ; recommandations avant le 15 décembre. Cet appel ne vise pas les étudiant·es de master. [Conditions et procédure, en anglais](https://histecon.fr/en/research_grants.html).
