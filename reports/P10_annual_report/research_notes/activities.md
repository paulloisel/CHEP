## Material for a programme / partnership spread

**Draft framing: research exchange at three scales.** The annual report can distinguish a recurring Paris reading seminar, a cross-centre research workshop, and a shared online seminar series. The June workshop’s retrospective account establishes discussion of research and prospective collaborations; it does not establish that new collaborations subsequently began, or how many people attended. The 2025 workshop is useful background but lies outside the reporting period. The [French recent-events alias](https://histecon.fr/fr/evenement_recents.html), P10-S217, agrees with the English page on both June 2026 dates.

The *Histoires* series offers a documented setting for collective reading across career stages. The annual programme includes colonial gender, climate risk, capitalism and visual culture, inheritance, deglobalisation and consumption. This is an editorial description of its listed subjects, not an evaluation of participant experience. The final publication could include two short participant-approved accounts of how a source or method changed after discussion; no such account is publicly available in the checked pages.

The [Economic History, Climate, and the Environment initiative](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/), P10-S207, identifies Cambridge, Paris and Harvard as its bases and links Sciences Po’s *La matière du politique* seminar. The latter’s [2025–26 archive](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/la-matiere-du-politique/), P10-S206, lists eight sessions around seas, oceans and coasts, with retrospective framing of the series. Record these separately as wider-network provision: the inspected page does not establish CHEP funding or co-organisation, and the retrospective introduction cannot prove delivery of every scheduled session. Do not add these eight to the fourteen core/shared records without widening and relabelling the denominator.

**Draft thematic connection, not a publication claim.** Lamotte’s abstract presents imperial legal orders through the labour, sexuality and efforts of subordinated people to protect family and improve their circumstances. Ghachem’s abstract links Saint-Domingue’s plantation regime to French financial pressures around the Mississippi Bubble. These two shared seminars can frame a short editorial question: how do archives of law and finance reveal people whose work sustains economic institutions? Their authors are visiting speakers in this evidence chain; neither seminar proves CHEP membership, CHEP funding of the research, or a publication date.

## Conflicts and editorial queries

1. **Junior-conference dates:** the [junior-colloquium index](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/les-formations/les-etudes-doctorales/le-colloque-junior/), P10-S205, says 26–27 November 2025. The detailed [event page](https://www.sciencespo.fr/recherche/en/events/conference-junior-invisible-actors-in-the-making-of-international-law/), P10-S204, and programme PDF agree on 27–28 November. Use 27–28 November provisionally, preserving the conflict in the register; request final programme confirmation.
2. **Programme version:** the PDF schedules a Tamar Herzog keynote, whereas the event page substitutes a roundtable and says her text will be read by Daniela Caglioti. The page also changes one panel speaker. Do not write that Herzog attended or delivered the keynote. Retain the cofunding credit from the PDF but verify the final programme before publication.
3. **Held status:** dated calendar entries and placement under “past” or “recent” are not automatically evidence that an event occurred. Seek convenor confirmation or a contemporaneous recap for ten core/shared records. The four confirmed records rely on explicit past-event wording, not page crawl dates.
4. **Current vs cached pages:** the web tool exposed cached/indexed retrievals. An older search snippet for CHEP recent events omitted the June 2026 records; opening the underlying URL returned them. This is a retrieval-version discrepancy, not proof of a site defect. No claim about the live website’s maintenance follows from it.
5. **Institutional attribution:** support for *Histoires* is explicit at series level; no session-specific expense or sponsor share is public. Workshop and joint-series co-organisation classifications should be confirmed with the academic convenor. The Visualizing roundtable’s classification as centre-organised is inferred from its official CHEP listing, not an explicit institutional credit.
