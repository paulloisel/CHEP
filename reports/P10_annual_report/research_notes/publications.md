### Intellectual substance for feature 1: institutions and everyday economic life

The accessible book blurb and contents support a feature explaining how prison records reveal everyday relations of power. Registers, dossiers and correspondence let Génard combine counting with the reconstruction of individual trajectories. The contents move through admission, wartime cohabitation, work and inequality, punishment, family ties and release. This establishes a connection between the social history of institutions and the material conditions of daily life; it does not license claims about every chapter's conclusions. A complementary sentence can acknowledge editorial labour: the *Tracés* issue treats authority as a social relation, examining how situations, language, material arrangements and unequal statuses sustain or challenge it. These are paraphrases of accessible descriptions, not claims to have read the complete book or interviewed its author. Sources P10-S105 and S112. No cover, photograph or quotation is cleared for reproduction; a text-only feature is ready for author/institutional review.

Root is combining this with separate Skoglund and Balguy research-in-progress features, which better balances career stages and topics than stretching the book list into three supposedly new monographs.
