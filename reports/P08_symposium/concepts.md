## P08-C1 — From compensation record to economic claim

**Distinct question:** How do administrative and legal records determine whose loss becomes an economically legible claim?

**Rationale (approximately 150 words).** CHEP offers a particularly useful setting for comparing the production of evidence. Jessica Balguy’s documented research concerns compensation to former slaveowners and colonial land; Rébecca Mathiot studies the recognition and compensation of maritime occupational risks. These are different historical injustices and institutional arrangements, not morally interchangeable cases. Their juxtaposition could expose how claimants, losses, proof and exclusions are constructed in records. CHEP’s Archives Notariales connection and Claire Lemercier’s comparative work provide a methodological bridge. A workshop would ask participants to annotate two short source dossiers, then test the same provisional coding questions against both. The shared exercise could uncover where comparison fails, which categories should remain specific, and what further evidence is needed. Its benefit would be usable feedback on unfinished research and a small methods note. It would complement existing readings and source seminars through paired analysis, rather than repeat a speaker’s presentation or assume demand for another general series.

**Audience and purpose:** proposed 12–16 participants, including Master’s and doctoral researchers, economic/social historians and a methods respondent. This is a research-development session, not a public showcase; size is an assumption, not a forecast.

**Questions:** Who created the document and for what decision? Who can claim and who cannot? What counts as proof? Does an award measure loss, entitlement, political power or something else? Which seemingly comparable fields cannot be combined?

**Participant profile:** Balguy and Mathiot as possible dossier presenters; Lemercier as possible methods respondent; one unnamed Master’s researcher as analytical respondent/co-editor. David Todd is a possible academic convenor. These are alternatives for discussion, not duties assigned by this report. If Mathiot cannot provide a suitable dossier, redesign with a willing junior presenter; do not retain a doctoral name merely as decoration.

**Format:** 3.5-hour local half-day; two 50-minute dossier sessions, small-group annotation, comparison and agreed next steps. Preparatory pack limited to eight pages, with contextual introductions and source-use permissions checked by the presenters. There is no expectation that the organising student independently transcribes difficult manuscripts.

**Likely output:** two corrected source cards; a two-page note on comparable and non-comparable categories; optional peer follow-up within four weeks. Contributors decide later whether a suitably anonymised version may be shared.

**Principal tradeoffs:** scholarly depth comes from a very narrow question; it cannot survey compensation history. Sensitive and unevenly accessible records need specialist framing. Offer French/English summaries and source glossaries; professional interpretation is an additional dependency if required. Planning envelope: **29 student hours**, **33 other contribution/support hours**; conditional zero incremental cash for the lean version, or optional refreshments/printing **€88–198**, with room and unmet access costs unknown. See the workplan for assumptions and gates.

**Evidence:** [CHEP Research Fellows](https://histecon.fr/en/research_fellows.html); [Research students 2026](https://histecon.fr/en/research_students.html); [Archives Notariales](https://www.histecon.fr/archivesnotariales/); candidate work sources in [participants.csv](participants.csv). Complement check: [Histoires de la vie économique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/histoires-de-la-vie-economique/), P08-CAL-S01. Fit is an **inference** from verified work, not evidence of willingness.

## P08-C2 — When does environmental change become economic loss?

**Distinct question:** What becomes visible—and disappears—when environmental change is reconstructed through prices, food records, inventories and physical measurements?

**Rationale (approximately 150 words).** The joint Economic History, Climate, and the Environment programme supplies a documented connection between Paris, Cambridge and Harvard. At Sciences Po, Emelyn Rude’s work on food and ecological sources and Giacomo Parrinello’s work on rivers and sediment suggest a focused historical conversation about evidence of loss. The purpose would be to compare how a changing environment enters records of ordinary economic life, without assuming that prices exhaust its significance. Participants could place a menu or food-market record beside a river-management or sediment record, identify scale and sampling problems, and develop questions that neither archive can answer alone. This would produce a source-pairing exercise and a short account of evidentiary limits. Existing environmental workshops and the La matière du politique seminar make a broad environmental symposium difficult to justify. A useful variant therefore needs an agreed, specific problem and must be developed with an existing programme’s convenor before becoming a separate event.

**Audience and purpose:** proposed 15–20 environmental/economic historians, doctoral researchers, and interested methods scholars. Research-development format; a short public-facing synthesis would be optional after review.

**Questions:** Does apparent scarcity reflect ecological change, classification or collection practice? Which spatial and temporal scales can be linked? How should historians record uncertainty when connecting physical evidence and human accounts? Whose losses are missing?

**Participant profile:** Rude and Parrinello as possible case presenters; Paul Warde as an optional partner respondent, subject to work/availability verification and an already-funded visit or separately supported participation. Select two junior projects after an academic convenor checks fit. An institutional profile retrieved for Rude is old; her current appointment and local presence remain unverified. No affiliation is inferred merely from a partner programme listing.

**Format:** a local half-day can test the question. A full day would allow three pairs of sources and a junior project clinic. International expansion is justified only if a third case changes the comparative question and partner staff share organisation; it is not required for intellectual credibility.

**Likely output:** three annotated source-pairing cards and a short “what this source cannot show” guide; no edited volume promised.

**Principal tradeoffs:** strong thematic connection, but greater conceptual breadth, non-local dependencies and overlap with existing activity. A full-day planning variant is estimated at **45 student hours** and **55 other hours**, with modelled optional cash **€506–957**, plus unresolved venue/access costs. A 90-minute session inside an existing seminar would be a safer first test if convenors prefer it.

**Evidence:** [Economic History, Climate, and the Environment](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/) (P08-S10); [Emelyn Rude](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/researcher/Emelyn%20Rude/89299.html) (P08-S11); [Giacomo Parrinello](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/researcher/Giacomo%20Parrinello/76291.html) (P08-S12); [Recent Centre Events](https://histecon.fr/en/events_recent.html) (P08-CAL-S02); [La matière du politique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/la-matiere-du-politique/) (P08-CAL-S04). Historical work is **verified** at source; contemporary attendance and demand are **unverified**.

## P08-C3 — Who gets to decide investment? Histories of public authority

**Distinct question:** How do new research projects identify the people and institutions authorised to direct investment, and what historical evidence tests those accounts?

**Rationale (approximately 150 words).** CHEP documents Chiara Cordelli’s 2025–2027 Senior Research Fellowship and participation in History and the Economic Life of the State. Jonathan Levy’s collaboration with Cordelli supplies a concrete intellectual connection, while Giulio Piumelli’s doctoral work on the Washington Consensus offers a possible emerging-project perspective. The aim would be a structured encounter between historical investigation and normative argument: each contributor would state one claim about investment authority, identify evidence that could revise it, and ask a junior respondent to test the link. A shared glossary would distinguish state ownership, public purpose, delegation and democratic control instead of treating them as synonyms. The value of collective discussion lies in exposing incompatible units and standards of proof. Because a nearby workshop already addresses economic planning in December 2026, this concept should remain a contingent follow-up about research design, not become another broad conference on planning or a presumption of collaboration with its organisers.

**Audience and purpose:** proposed 12–18 historians, political theorists and doctoral researchers. Work-in-progress clinic; not a manifesto, policy recommendation or public debate promising consensus.

**Questions:** Is the unit of explanation an institution, an instrument or a decision? When does public financing entail public authority? How do researchers distinguish historical description from a normative criterion? Which sources reveal contestation rather than only official intent?

**Participant profile:** Levy and Cordelli as possible senior interlocutors, Piumelli as possible project presenter, plus one further doctoral project chosen through a later fit check. LIEPP/CEE/TIERED is a possible programme-coordination route only; no endorsement or partnership is established.

**Format:** two senior/junior project pairs, each given a ten-minute claim, ten-minute evidence challenge and moderated revision. Prefer one half-day after the December 2026 programme is assessed. A public international version is unjustified at present; compare its costs only as a contingency.

**Likely output:** a two-page glossary and individual revised research questions; a cross-project reading list after consent. Publication is a later decision, not the default deliverable.

**Principal tradeoffs:** direct intellectual fit but high overlap risk, dependence on senior participants, and possible dominance of abstract theory. Student presenters must open each pair and co-author the output. A local half-day could use the C1 resource ceiling, subject to entirely new confirmations; a four-visitor European extension is estimated at **65 student hours**, **95 other hours**, and modelled cash **€1,908.50–3,932.50** plus unknown venue/access/fee costs; transatlantic travel and recording are excluded.

**Evidence:** [CHEP Research Visitors](https://histecon.fr/en/research_visitors.html) (P08-S13); [Organisation](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html) (P08-S04); individual work records in [participants.csv](participants.csv); [What is Economic Planning? Historical and Contemporary Perspectives](https://www.sciencespo.fr/liepp/fr/actualites/what-is-economic-planning-historical-and-contemporary-perspectives/) (P08-CAL-S03). The connection is documented; the proposed format and its usefulness are **inferred**.
