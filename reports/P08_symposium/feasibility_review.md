# P08 feasibility review: local source-comparison pilot

as_of_date: 2026-11-15  
Status: planning advice; every estimate below is an analyst judgement, not a quote, CHEP commitment, or evidence of available resources. No external contact or booking occurred. This bounded review applies the shared brief and P08; it does not execute another prompt.

## Main finding

A 3.5-hour local workshop can fit the five-student-hours-per-week scenario if preparation is spread over six weeks, the student works from two short source dossiers supplied by presenters, and administrative and academic support are explicitly allocated. The proposed 29 hours are plausible but have little tolerance for translation, bespoke archival research, late presenter changes or public-event production. Protect at least three setup hours for the student's substantive source response and synthesis; do not let all 21 setup hours become administration.

Use 12–16 people as an untested working assumption, not a forecast or attendance promise. Neither room capacity nor demand has been confirmed. Two presenters, one additional respondent and one convenor are enough; the coordinating Master's student can fill the second respondent role if willing and academically supported. A separate student respondent would add their preparation and attendance to other-support time.

Keep a weekly time log; at four hours of actual use, the convenor must absorb or defer any work that would push the week over five.

International range assumes four Europe-based visitors and one night each. It does not cover transatlantic travel; a Harvard visitor needs a fresh quote and potentially extra nights. It is therefore a comparison scenario, not an all-network symposium budget. No international version should proceed on an unconfirmed grant or an expectation that participants will pay their own travel. A single remote respondent could reduce travel costs, but hybrid delivery still requires technical support, an accessible platform and a revised timetable; it is not included in the recommended pilot.

## Gates and stop/adapt rules

1. **T−6 scope gate:** an academic convenor accepts intellectual ownership and confirms the workshop complements existing provision. Confirm that 29 event hours fit the student's broader duties. Otherwise offer the source worksheet within an existing seminar instead.
2. **T−4 resource gate:** room access, registration mechanism, administrative coverage, presenter availability and funding route are resolved. Zero cash is allowed only with explicit in-kind provision. Unknown essential access costs stop the event until resourced; removing accommodations is not a fallback.
3. **T−2 content gate:** two short, usable dossiers and one prepared student response exist; source reuse conditions are checked. If only one dossier survives, convert to a 90-minute single-dossier session and recompute workload rather than advertising the full programme.
4. **T−1 capacity gate:** actual weekly hours remain within five; no full recording, press package, elaborate catering or additional papers are added. Room troubleshooting belongs to the host/admin support, allowing the student to present and discuss.
5. **Afterwards:** continue only if participants report specific changes to interpretation or research design and at least one voluntary follow-on exchange. A methods note and honest decision to stop are useful results; an edited volume or standing series is not promised.

## Equity and access conditions

Make pre-reading short (suggested total 6–8 pages including context and worksheet), readable in accessible formats and available at least a week in advance. Invite language needs when planning and permit French or English discussion where the convenor can support it; do not promise simultaneous interpretation. Identify the historical and ethical differences between dossiers explicitly, especially where compensation to former slaveowners is placed beside compensation for workers' injuries. Similar archival labels do not imply equivalent harms or claims.

Students should choose whether to present; participation must not depend on sharing sensitive or unfinished thesis material. Attribute intellectual contributions with consent and offer a chance to correct the note. Do not require unpaid student reception duty during their presentation, charge an attendance fee to make the budget balance, or assume personal research grants can cover event expenses.

## Limits

This is a feasibility audit of the proposed format, not a survey of CHEP's internal resources. Venue rules, purchasing thresholds, staff allocation, participant willingness, specialist accessibility charges and honorarium policy require institutional information. No factual claims about those resources are made here. Research evidence and candidate fit are maintained separately by the main P08 report.
