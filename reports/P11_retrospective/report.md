## Decision brief

**What we learned.** A fifth anniversary in 2027 is defensible only when named as five years since **establishment in 2022**. CHEP also has a documented **5 June 2019 launch**; its fifth anniversary fell in 2024. The [official launch-workshop account](https://histecon.fr/en/spatial.html) and [Sciences Po institutional overview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/research/centre-for-history-and-economics-in-paris-chep/) support this distinction. The precise establishment day remains unknown. Jonathan Levy is identified publicly as **Coordinator**, not “Director,” on the [organisation page](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html).

The English and French [CHEP publication lists](https://histecon.fr/en/publications.html) display 18 edition entries representing 14 underlying works after linking four translation pairs. They are a selected participant showcase, not a production census. Our [bibliography](bibliography.csv) accounts for that entire displayed list, adds original editions and selected chapter/reception links, and expands three junior-author bibliographies. It distinguishes publication, affiliation and institutional support. The final inventory counts and exclusions are documented in [coverage.md](coverage.md).

**What CHEP can do.** Use a short, text-led digital retrospective as a reading guide: four thematic conversations, six substantial cards, an 18-work selection and a downloadable evidence inventory. It can help newcomers find research across languages and formats and give an optional discussion a common starting point. These are anticipated benefits; demand has not been measured.

## Editorial case and institutional chronology

An anniversary publication should explain how scholars investigate economic life, rather than imply that an institution produced every book on its website. The source list is especially useful for showing different scales: economic concepts, property and sovereignty, collective organisation, and individual lives reconstructed from archives. It is less useful as a measure of output or impact. A title may appear because its author participated in CHEP activities, and its research may long precede the Paris centre.

The chronology makes that distinction consequential. The [2019 workshop report](https://histecon.fr/en/spatial.html) explicitly presents a launch; current institutional accounts subsequently identify establishment in 2022. These are compatible stages of development, not dates to average or resolve by choosing whichever fits an anniversary title. The recommended wording is **“Five years since CHEP’s establishment, 2022–2027: histories of economic life.”** For now, every public-facing page should carry “Preparation draft; evidence checked through 15 November 2026.” The [anniversary assessment](anniversary_basis.md) records the intermediate institutional evidence and remaining question.

Our main commemorative period is 1 January 2022–15 November 2026. The bibliography also covers the collaboration prehistory, 1 January 2019–31 December 2021, plus explicitly labelled earlier originals or featured contextual works. January 1 is an editorial boundary, not a claimed birthday. A 2027 final freeze remains to be agreed; the draft makes no claim to cover five completed establishment years or future outputs.

## Corpus and attribution decisions

The corpus has three layers. First is every edition displayed on both CHEP publication pages, with publisher or institutional metadata checks. Second is a deliberate junior expansion: Jessica Balguy, Joan Chaker and Francesco Giosuè, using public author bibliographies and associated publishers. Third is a small, explicitly selective set of original editions, two chapters within a featured volume and a reception article. It is comprehensive as a transcription of the checked CHEP list, and source-bounded for the three-author expansion. It is **not** a comprehensive bibliography of all current or former Paris affiliates. The remaining person/category queue is part of the deliverable.

Books, coauthored studies, edited volumes, short monographs, journal articles, chapters, public essays and reviews retain distinct types. Theses have their own register and are excluded from published-text totals. Unpublished or forthcoming material beyond the cutoff is excluded. Wider Cambridge and Harvard membership does not itself qualify a work. The [current associate page](https://histecon.fr/en/research_associates.html) supplies Paris relationship evidence but usually no start date: current listing cannot retrospectively establish affiliation during a book’s preparation.

Use separate labels for **featured by CHEP**, **published during a documented affiliation**, **earlier work by a later affiliate**, and **explicitly supported by CHEP**. Publication during affiliation is still not proof of production there. No positive CHEP funding claim is established for the inventoried works by the inspected evidence. This is an evidence limit, not a claim that support never occurred. In particular, the [acknowledgements to *A World of Public Debts*](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bfm:978-3-030-48794-2/1), pp. xxix–xxx, describe a pre-existing project and support from other institutions, including a Cambridge meeting. Paris, Cambridge and the Sciences Po host should not be conflated.

Affiliation timing can be genuinely conflicting. Anatole Le Bras’s past-fellow dates differ from the home-page wording and his publisher biography. That dispute is retained in the register; it does not undermine the verified existence of *Aliénés*. Elsa Génard’s 2025 book is listed separately from her documented 2021–2022 fellowship. For current junior researchers, a 2026 page label likewise cannot turn their earlier scholarship into CHEP-supported output. These distinctions permit celebration without assigning institutional credit that the evidence cannot sustain.

## Four conversations, with different kinds of evidence

**Capitalism, credit and public power** places conceptual history alongside concrete mechanisms of debt. The [Todd–Yates chapter abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-48794-2_4) connects public borrowing to investment publics and citizenship; its joint authorship is a documented research connection. Other juxtapositions in the draft are editorial comparisons, not claims of influence. An edited volume, a short article and a theoretical book offer different routes into the conversation.

**Property, empire and environments** asks how economic relations acquire spatial forms. [Alexia Yates’s *Real Estate and Global Urban History*](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108850551) supplies a property-based framework, while [Sunil Amrith’s *Unruly Waters*](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/290609/unruly-waters-by-amrith-sunil/9780141982632) opens an environmental dimension. This connection is a proposed reading path; it is not evidence of collaboration. Retaining the 2018 original date behind Amrith’s 2020 paperback prevents a reprint from becoming a new research accomplishment.

**Solidarity and social institutions** makes room for both collective action and institutional constraints. The [publisher description of Delalande’s English translation](https://otherpress.com/product/struggle-and-mutual-aid-9781635420104/) offers an entry into labour internationalism. Génard and Le Bras bring different archives and forms of confinement into the draft. The purpose is to pose comparative questions about dependence, support and authority, not to suggest identical experiences or one shared argument.

**Evidence, lives and scales** connects the construction of historical data with choices about whose lives become visible. [Lemercier and Zalc’s methods guide](https://www.upress.virginia.edu/title/5168/) is valuable because it can be used by a student reader, not only admired as an achievement. Rothschild’s family history and Giosuè’s shorter public scholarship broaden the possible forms of engagement. Each card identifies whether its basis was a publisher description, author summary, abstract or accessible text. None represents a full-book review.

## Selection, omissions and publication rights

The [selection register](selection.csv) gives 18 underlying works a specific editorial role; six receive full cards. Criteria are Paris connection, verified metadata, adequate evidence for any argument described, contribution to a thematic question, and variety of form, language and career stage. The result is not a ranking of quality. Multiple editions share a work ID, while a chapter has its own work ID and a parent-volume note. Counting both a collection and a chapter is legitimate bibliographic description but should never be presented as two independent institutional achievements.

The selection remains book-heavy because the centre’s public showcase is book-heavy. Junior articles and a student essay materially improve it, but three checked junior authors cannot stand for the entire community. Coverage of other students, former fellows, visitors, environmental research and work in languages beyond French and English remains uneven. A future editor should complete the named queue before using the bibliography for membership-wide claims. Do not infer absence of student research from absence of a public record.

The draft reproduces no covers, photographs, book pages or invented interviews. It uses original summaries and links. Each record carries a rights/status note: public availability or an open-access URL does not automatically license illustrations, long quotations or republication of a full text. French titles and accents are preserved; an English orientation is provided. A fully bilingual edition would need additional translation and review time, estimated separately in the [format comparison](celebration_options.md).

## Feasibility and next work package

The digital edition is a bounded editorial project. A small booklet is estimated at 44 student preparation/delivery hours plus one archiving hour and €220–340, while the half-day gathering needs 53 preparation/delivery hours plus one archiving hour and €250–500 under local-speaker, free-room assumptions. Those are planning allowances, not supplier quotations, and exclude travel or honoraria. The more elaborate formats offer different benefits but place greater demands on coordination and support. Their lead times and assumptions are specified in [celebration_options.md](celebration_options.md).

The first five-hour student package should contain a one-page anniversary decision sheet, a list of unresolved affiliation dates, and a proposed editorial freeze. The next twenty hours refine metadata, read and check selected material, incorporate one academic review, and prepare an accessible text-and-link release. If an academic sponsor or web owner is unavailable, retain a reviewable internal file and defer public release. An optional discussion should be approved as its own five-hour addition, with participant involvement unconfirmed until explicitly agreed.

## Methods and limitations

This run read `Summary.md` and used its four institutional starting points, independently of other prompt reports. Research combined official navigation, French/English comparisons, targeted discovery searches, publisher pages, institutional bibliographies and repository checks. Indexed/cached retrieval ages are recorded where exposed; an access date is not proof of live-site freshness. Failed retrievals were not interpreted as broken public pages. No bulk crawl, accounts, outreach or publication was used.

Metadata-only items receive no invented argument. Source IDs, direct URLs and missing-value labels make the inventory auditable. Some edition relationships rely on indexed national-library or author records whose direct targets could not be retrieved; these are flagged for rechecking. Internal evidence is still needed for the exact establishment day, affiliation boundaries, institutional support, editorial authority and actual available resources. The output is a concrete preparation draft with a defensible checked corpus, not an institutional history certified by CHEP.
