## Three further entry points from the junior-author selection

**Jessica Balguy, *Indemniser l’esclavage en 1848 ?* (2020), P11-JW001.** This edition of historical debates brings disputes over compensation into the reading list through sources rather than a conventional monograph. It is earlier scholarship by a later-listed CHEP fellow. [Karthala, bibliographic record and presentation](https://www.karthala.com/accueil/3371-indemniser-l-esclavage-en-1848-debats-dans-l-empire-francais-du-xixe-siecle-9782811128036.html); P11-JS006–007.

**Joan Chaker, *Debt, Domicide, and the Politics of Method in Urban Studies* (17 June 2026), P11-JW010.** Chaker argues for studying housing destruction and financialisation as interconnected processes operating across borders. The introduction makes this a useful methodological counterpoint to historical studies of property. Its publisher biography gives a Harvard affiliation; publication during CHEP affiliation is unverified. [Urban Matters, article introduction](https://urbanmattersjournal.com/debt-domicide-and-the-politics-of-method-in-urban-studies-towards-a-structural-analysis-from-palestine-to-lebanon-and-beyond/); P11-JS016–018.

**Francesco Giosuè, *The Historiographical and Political Reception of the Revolutionary Jean-Joseph Mounier* (11 August 2025), P11-JW011.** This public scholarly essay examines the political categories used to interpret Mounier. It offers a student-authored example of making a historical debate accessible in a short format. The essay predates the documented 2026 CHEP student listing. [Age of Revolutions, full essay](https://ageofrevolutions.com/2025/08/11/the-historiographical-and-political-reception-of-the-revolutionary-jean-joseph-mounier/); P11-JS019–020.
