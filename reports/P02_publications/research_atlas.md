# Research atlas: seven conversations across the checked corpus

**as_of_date: 2026-11-15. Draft analytic aid, not an official CHEP research taxonomy.** All sources below were accessed on that date; full source titles and retrieval qualifications are in [sources.csv](sources.csv). Work IDs resolve in [publications.csv](publications.csv). These clusters overlap and should not be summed. They describe possible scholarly connections, not inferred influence, personal relationships or willingness to participate.

The atlas uses publisher/author synopses, abstracts, introductions and selected open essays. It does not pretend that all 183 provisional works were read. Much of the larger bibliography is metadata-only. The stated reading basis applies to each anchor; an editorial question is a proposed use, not a finding about unmet demand.

## 1. What is capital, and who controls investment?

**Anchors:** Jonathan Levy, *The Real Economy* (P02-W002), and *Capital as Process and the History of Capitalism*; Chiara Cordelli, *What Is the Wrong of Capitalism?* (P02-W088); their coauthored *The Ethics of Global Capital Mobility* (P02-W089); Liane Hewitt, *Monopoly Menace* (P02-W078).

Levy's [article abstract and available text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-history-review/article/capital-as-process-and-the-history-of-capitalism/6F9E5C1C35563B66388E2896444178A9) centre prospective valuation and investment. Cordelli's [2025 abstract/introduction](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/what-is-the-wrong-of-capitalism/CD320C659A33C006C6562D363FD5D954) makes collective control of social futures a normative problem. Hewitt's [published dissertation summary](https://doi.org/10.1017/eso.2024.38) examines the political transformation of cartels from accepted instruments into prohibited arrangements. Reading basis: article abstracts/introductions and Levy's accessible text, not the entire dissertations or books. Sources P02-SL010, P02-SC016–017, P02-SR026.

**Editorial connection:** compare historical forms of investment authority with arguments about who should exercise it. The Cordelli–Levy article supplies documented coauthorship; no other collaboration is inferred. **Discussion question:** when a rule changes the movement or organisation of capital, whose capacity to shape the future changes?

## 2. Credit, public power and the boundaries of trust

**Anchors:** Francesca Trivellato, *The Promise and Peril of Credit* / *Juifs et capitalisme* (P02-W005); Nicolas Barreyre and Nicolas Delalande, *A World of Public Debts* (P02-W011); Levy, *Freaks of Fortune* and *Accounting for Profit and the History of Capital*.

Trivellato's [publisher account](https://press.princeton.edu/books/ebook/9780691185378/the-promise-and-peril-of-credit-pdf) traces a false story about Jewish invention of financial instruments and its implications for commercial society. The [public-debt collection](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-48794-2) treats borrowing as politically organised, while Levy's [article abstract](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/677977) examines historically changing accounting practices. Reading basis: publisher descriptions and article abstract; no claim to all chapters. Sources P02-SF016, P02-SF022, P02-SL005, P02-SL011.

**Editorial connection:** legal authority, accounting categories and cultural exclusion offer different ways of asking how credit becomes credible. They should not be collapsed into a single explanation. **Discussion question:** which archives reveal the boundary between a legitimate financial claim and an illegitimate one? A short reading selection could juxtapose a documented transaction with the language used to justify it.

## 3. Slavery, empire and contested property

**Anchors:** Jessica Balguy, *Indemniser l’esclavage en 1848 ?* (P02-W071) and *L’abolition de l’esclavage de 1848*; M’hamed Oualdi, *Un esclave entre deux empires* (P02-W006); David Todd, *A Velvet Empire* / *Un empire de velours* (P02-W008); Hewitt, *The World in Blocs* (P02-W079).

Balguy's [BnF essay](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/labolition-lesclavage-1848) connects abolition with contested compensation, labour, land and citizenship. Oualdi's [original-book publisher description](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/a-slave-between-empires/9780231191869/) follows Husayn's estate and competing claims around it. Todd's [French publisher account](https://www.editionsladecouverte.fr/un_empire_de_velours-9782348073359) highlights informal imperial power; Hewitt's [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1740022822000262) supplies a different, twentieth-century imperial regionalism. Reading basis: BnF public text, publisher descriptions and article abstract. Sources P02-SR035, P02-SF028, P02-SF011, P02-SQ001; consult the register if a source's canonical route redirects.

**Editorial connection:** compare compensation, inheritance, jurisdiction and international economic organisation without erasing their different places and periods. **Discussion question:** who could make an enforceable property claim across changing political boundaries? A junior-researcher contribution could be a comparative source note, not an assertion that these authors share one account of empire.

## 4. Confinement, care and everyday institutional authority

**Anchors:** Elsa Génard, *Sous les verrous* (P02-W001); Anatole Le Bras, *Aliénés* (P02-W004) and *Les lieux du soin* (P02-W030); their shared work on family ties and disciplinary institutions; Cordelli, *The Privatized State* (P02-W084).

Génard's [project account](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehppf/Elsa_Genard.html) and Le Bras's [publisher synopsis](https://www.cnrseditions.fr/catalogue/histoire/alienes/) make institutional records a means of reconstructing relationships extending beyond prison or asylum walls. The [indexed Pouvoirs abstract](https://droit.cairn.info/revue-pouvoirs-2025-5-page-29?lang=fr) addresses changes in psychiatric care and coercion; direct retrieval failed, so interpretation is limited. Cordelli's [book synopsis](https://www.chiaracordelli.com/new-page) raises the separate normative question of legitimate authority when public functions are outsourced. Sources P02-SR040, P02-SF007, P02-SR043, P02-SC009; reading basis is project description/synopsis and the indexed abstract.

**Editorial connection:** contrast lived relationships inside institutions with the allocation of public responsibilities. Génard and Le Bras's coauthored [introduction](https://doi.org/10.3917/lms1.279.0003) is a documented collaboration, counted once. **Discussion question:** what changes when an institution moves care, punishment or responsibility outside its walls?

## 5. Solidarity, civil society and unequal resources

**Anchors:** Delalande, *La Lutte et l’Entraide* / *Struggle and Mutual Aid* (P02-W007); Pierre François and Claire Lemercier, *Sociologie historique du capitalisme* (P02-W010); Rob Reich, Chiara Cordelli and Lucy Bernholz, *Philanthropy in Democratic Societies*; Levy's chapter on nonprofit philanthropy.

Delalande's [institutional interview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/research/cogito/home/what-remains-of-the-workers-internationals/?lang=en) is catalogued in P02-SF034. His account of solidarity asks how collective action was financed and sustained. The [Chicago collection's description and contents](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/P/bo23530413.html) frame philanthropy through its institutional power and democratic justification. François/Lemercier's [publisher contents](https://www.editionsladecouverte.fr/sociologie_historique_du_capitalisme-9782348059834) provide a wider history of labour and economic institutions. Reading basis: interview, publisher descriptions and contents. Sources P02-SF034, P02-SL030, P02-SF014.

**Editorial connection:** mutual aid and philanthropy can be compared as ways of organising resources without assuming identical political purposes. **Discussion question:** when does collective financing sustain equal participation, and when does it create dependence on donors or intermediaries? The volume and individual chapters remain separate bibliographic units, not separate institutional achievements.

## 6. Climate, infrastructure and the historical production of vulnerability

**Anchors:** Sunil Amrith, *Unruly Waters* (P02-W014); Alexia Yates, *Real Estate and Global Urban History* (P02-W009); Giacomo Parrinello, *Times of Drought* (P02-W181); Dev Patel, *The Pace of Climate Change* (P02-W182); Levy, *Snow in Houston* (P02-W179); Cordelli and Melissa Lane, *Climate Change: NOMOS LXVII* (P02-W087).

The three fully read public essays offer immediately usable contrasts. [Parrinello](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/climate-loss/po/index.html) explains how intensive water use made the Po economy vulnerable; [Patel](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/climate-loss/pace_of_change/index.html) discusses the difficulty of detecting gradual environmental change in Bangladesh; [Levy](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/climate-loss/houston_snow/index.html) considers climate and historical consciousness through Houston. The [NOMOS publisher description](https://nyupress.org/9781479842094/climate-change/) adds questions of justice and responsibility. Sources P02-SL018–020, P02-SC014. Amrith and Yates are further publisher-description anchors, not additional texts claimed as fully read.

**Editorial connection:** infrastructure, perception and political responsibility work at different timescales. **Discussion question:** how does a long history of economic adaptation become a present constraint? The [joint environmental programme](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/) documents a Paris connection, but does not make every partner publication a CHEP output.

## 7. Connecting lives, numbers and institutions

**Anchors:** Emma Rothschild, *An Infinite History* / *De proche en proche* (P02-W003); Claire Lemercier and Claire Zalc, *Quantitative Methods in the Humanities* (P02-W012); Paul-André Rosental, *Population, the state, and national grandeur* (P02-W013); Génard's work on prison statistics.

Rothschild's [author companion site](https://www.infinitehistory.org/fr/) makes family and social connections visible. Lemercier and Zalc's [companion project](https://quanthum.hypotheses.org/about) explains the scope of their translated and revised methods book. Rosental's [publisher-hosted introduction](https://www.peterlang.com/document/1063068), recorded as P02-SF020, examines the relationship between demographic knowledge and state projects. Génard's coauthored [article](https://doi.org/10.1017/ahss.2019.94) is included as a promising metadata-level methods connection; its full argument was not read in this run. Sources P02-SF020, P02-SF024, P02-SF032–033, P02-SR021.

**Editorial connection:** numbers and networks are both research tools and historically produced categories. **Discussion question:** when does changing the unit of analysis—from individual file to family, institution or population—change the historical explanation? A small source exercise could pair a register entry with a table, explicitly documenting what each makes invisible.

## Editorial use and guardrails

Begin with three selections, not seven new events. Each should contain two or three readable texts, a 100-word explanation, an access-status label and one question. Ask an academic counterpart to check the interpretation and choice of texts before any public use. Publication counts and citation prestige do not rank research quality. The atlas's uneven density reflects the chosen research batches and public access; it is not evidence that other affiliates lack relevant work. No attendance, demand or willingness to convene has been inferred.
