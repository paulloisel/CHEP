# CHEP publication census and research atlas — bounded first release

**as_of_date: 2026-11-15. Research and proposals only; no material published or messages sent.** All web access dates are 15 November 2026. Source titles, owners, dates and retrieval qualifications are in [sources.csv](sources.csv).

## Decision brief

**What did we learn?** CHEP's English and French publication displays contain **18 editions representing 14 works by 15 named authors/editors**. Four translations account for the difference. The display is a curated book selection, not a bibliography of the community's scholarship. The expanded catalogue contains **188 text records representing 183 provisional distinct works**, covering 20 of the 39 documented scholarly identities in this roster. It includes articles, chapters, reviews, public essays and interviews. These are bounded research-inventory totals, **not CHEP-funded output totals or a complete lifetime census**. Of the records, 170 have verified metadata at the stated source level, 16 retain material metadata uncertainty, one has an inferred original/translation relationship, and one retains conflicting dating. See [publications.csv](publications.csv), [cohort.csv](cohort.csv) and the [two publication displays](https://histecon.fr/en/publications.html), [French version](https://histecon.fr/fr/publications.html).

Seven thematic connections in [research_atlas.md](research_atlas.md) connect economic categories, political authority, slavery and empire, confinement, solidarity, climate and historical methods. The atlas identifies what was actually read, rather than attributing arguments from titles alone. The [organisation page](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html) calls Jonathan Levy **Coordinator**; that is the title used here.

## Scope and what was completed

The census has no arbitrary publication-number ceiling. It covers texts published by the cutoff, retaining available career dates rather than restricting everything to CHEP's establishment. The earliest known underlying work represented is from 2003; the represented editions run from 2006 to 2026. The scope consists of: all items on both CHEP publication pages; expanded bibliography batches for **Jessica Balguy, Elsa Génard, Anatole Le Bras, Liane Hewitt, Jonathan Levy and Chiara Cordelli**; and two targeted Paris-linked public essays by Giacomo Parrinello and Dev Patel. These categories define the practical stopping boundary. The remaining careers are explicitly queued, not presumed empty.

The roster contains **33 core identities** with documented Paris roles, affiliations or support relationships, and **six wider featured-only participants**. “Core” includes current and former fellows, research associates, students, a visitor, a scientific-council member and a supported non-residential fellow; it does not mean permanent employment or residence in Paris. The six wider identities are Emma Rothschild, David Todd, Pierre François, Nicolas Barreyre, Claire Zalc and Sunil Amrith. Their careers were not imported wholesale. Paul-André Rosental's [Sciences Po profile](https://www.sciencespo.fr/recherche/en/directory/rosental-paul-andre/) explicitly identifies a CHEP scientific-council role, so he belongs in the core despite the inaccessible committee page. Roles and uncertainty remain visible in [cohort.csv](cohort.csv).

All 20 names on the accessible English/French associate directories were checked for cohort inclusion. The [host's CHEP page](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/research/centre-for-history-and-economics-in-paris-chep/) adds Joan Chaker and four scholarship holders; the narrower [current-fellows page](https://histecon.fr/en/research_fellows.html) displays Jessica Balguy. Wider Cambridge/Harvard membership was excluded unless a Paris connection or CHEP-featured work justified inclusion.

The six expansion batches use different evidence: Le Bras's dated institutional bibliography, Génard's visible HAL bibliography, Balguy's institutional CV plus primary additions, Hewitt's named publication venues, Levy's older institutional-hosted CV plus updates, and Cordelli's exposed author lists and accessible outlet archive. ## Reuse and maintenance

The useful unit is the work with linked versions, not a copied citation in several separate documents. `record_id` identifies the row; `work_id` identifies a reconciled text; `edition_or_version_id` identifies the particular manifestation. `source_ids` resolves to direct URLs, source titles and access dates. `person_ids` contains only documented cohort members; complete bylines retain outside coauthors without promoting them to CHEP affiliates. [record_crosswalk.csv](record_crosswalk.csv) preserves input-to-final reconciliation.

For a website selection, filter on featured status, type and review readiness. For an annual reporting period, filter edition dates, then inspect dated affiliation evidence and unresolved flags. For speaker preparation, use the atlas questions and content basis; publication counts supply no evidence of willingness, availability or teaching suitability. For a retrospective, use work IDs to avoid counting translations twice and distinguish earlier scholarship from the institutional period. These are reusable views of the same inventory, not execution of the other prompt workstreams.

## Methods and limitations

`Summary.md` contained the four starting URLs and no additional substantive brief. This run applied the shared source map independently; no other report was required. English/French navigation, aliases, relevant author lists, journal/publisher pages and repositories were inspected. The robots and sitemap routes were attempted; retrieval limitations led to targeted requests rather than automated bulk crawling. Search results served as leads, and unresolved publisher-indexed evidence is explicitly identified. No access restrictions were bypassed.

The web tool frequently returned indexed/cached representations; access on 15 November does not establish that the live site had exactly that state. Crawl dates and footer years were not treated as publication dates. No dated archival snapshot was obtained. The final register has **191 source records for 181 distinct URLs**, including failed targets and repeated sources used by separate batches. Retrieval failures do not demonstrate universally broken links.

The main limits are unresolved roster branches, uneven career expansion, inaccessible current CVs and incomplete outlet pagination. Three first-publication years remain unknown, and 16 Cordelli rows retain conservative `unverified` metadata status despite inspected author-list evidence of existence. [supplementary.csv](supplementary.csv) keeps 14 thesis, non-text, forthcoming and unresolved items outside the published-text totals. Missing open-access links mean “not verified here,” not “no lawful copy exists.” No covers or long quotations are supplied. The result is usable research infrastructure with a transparent completion queue, not a performance ranking or an institutional output claim.
