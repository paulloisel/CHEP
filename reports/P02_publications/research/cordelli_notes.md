## Atlas evidence suitable for the final report

1. **Democracy, state capacity, and privatization.** The [author's book synopsis](https://www.chiaracordelli.com/new-page) frames privatization as a problem of legitimate political authority, including prisons, welfare administration, and financial governance. The [EJPE abstract](https://www.ejpe.org/journal/article/view/808) stresses how separate acts of outsourcing can accumulate into structural dependence. These are useful bridges to historical research on state formation and coercive institutions, but cross-author intellectual connections are editorial analysis, not proof of influence.
2. **Capital, investment, and the future.** The [2025 APSR abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/what-is-the-wrong-of-capitalism/CD320C659A33C006C6562D363FD5D954) moves normative criticism toward investment and citizens' loss of collective authorship over social futures. The [Cordelli–Levy APSR abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/ethics-of-global-capital-mobility/EE8492BB3F517B642050C4319CA5D41E) evaluates which capital movements warrant protection or restriction, with monetary reform part of its proposal. This is a verified coauthorship connecting political philosophy to economic history; it predates their present documented Paris roles.
3. **Climate and intergenerational responsibility.** The [NYU description of Climate Change](https://nyupress.org/9781479842094/climate-change/) identifies questions about welfare economics, the character of climate injustice, and responsibility across persons and generations. Cordelli and Melissa Lane are coeditors; the volume appeared March 2026. Pair it analytically with environmental/economic histories, without representing it as CHEP-organised research or attributing every chapter's argument to the editors.
4. **Civil society, philanthropy, and unequal opportunity.** The [Chicago volume description and contents](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/P/bo23530413.html) ground a cluster about philanthropy's institutional power and democratic legitimacy. Its introduction and Cordelli chapter are separate contributions inside one edited container. Publisher abstracts on friendship, relational goods, and hiring provide further leads, but several OUP pages were only available in publisher-indexed excerpts and should receive a fuller retrieval before extended interpretation.
