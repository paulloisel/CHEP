# CHEP: organisation, people and institutional relationships

**P01 research draft — as_of_date: 2026-11-15 (America/Los_Angeles).** All sources were accessed on that date. This report executes P01 only. It uses `Summary.md` and the shared source map; no other report was required. Public material was researched without contacting anyone or changing a website.

## Decision brief

**What we learned.** CHEP is based at the Sciences Po Centre for History (CHSP). Its present overview distinguishes collaboration beginning in **2019** from establishment in **2022**. The most directly relevant retrieved organisation page calls Jonathan Levy **Coordinator**, corroborated by the current opened Sciences Po overview. It credits Mary-Rose Cheadle with coordination with Cambridge and Harvard, and Amy Price with website work. These are documented functions, not an evidenced management chain. [CHEP Organisation](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html); [Sciences Po CHEP overview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/research/centre-for-history-and-economics-in-paris-chep/).

The directory contains **72 distinct identities, 126 sourced role assertions and 22 organisational entities**, with current, historical, host-support, partner and participation categories separated. These totals are **not current CHEP membership counts**. The retrievable Paris associate list names 20 people; four doctoral scholarship holders are explicitly labelled 2026 by the host. Important uncertainties remain: the present Paris executive-committee roster, Cambridge's full current governance/support roster, the exact coordinator transition, and several dated affiliations. [Research Associates](https://histecon.fr/en/research_associates.html); [French host overview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/centre-for-history-and-economics-paris-chep/).

**What CHEP can do with it.** Reuse the same person and role IDs for a directory, annual-report attribution and introductions across research interests. Publish a current view only after resolving the entries flagged for confirmation; retain a separate historical view. The [organisation and partnership maps](organigram.md) already provide a reviewable prototype.

**What the student can deliver next.** The three proposed actions below form one small four-week package. Estimates assume **five student hours per week**, existing office/software access and **no confirmed discretionary budget**; they do not describe CHEP's actual resources.

The decision needed is which institutional function owns category/status decisions and which owns maintenance; no named individual is assigned a commitment.

## Institutional explanation

### Paris and its host

The strongest current host statement locates CHEP at CHSP. The French Sciences Po page also preserves a broader CHSP/CSO account, while minutes of the Sciences Po scientific council on 10 March 2020 explicitly describe both units as bases of the Paris initiative. The map therefore distinguishes the current CHSP host relationship from the documented earlier CSO/CHSP configuration. It does not imply that every member or administrative employee of either unit belongs to CHEP. [French CHEP overview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/centre-for-history-and-economics-paris-chep/); [2020 minutes, section 10, pp. 14–15](https://www.sciencespo.fr/sites/default/files/cd_cp/cs/2020/2020_03_10_CSFR.pdf).

The host's support page identifies administration, finance, communications, events, editorial and research-support functions. Those entries are included as **host-unit context**. Public evidence does not establish their CHEP time allocation, budget authority or responsibility for a particular proposed project. Anna Egea is separately credited for the wider Paris economic-history agenda, whose checked entries run through June 2025; that does not confirm her ongoing 2026 CHEP remit. [CHSP direction and support](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/the-centre/research-directorate-and-support-team/); [CHEP agenda](https://histecon.fr/fr/agenda.html).

The French host overview names Marie-Emmanuelle Chessel, Thierry Mayer, Paul-André Rosental and Emma Rothschild as contributors to organising activities. This wording is retained as collaboration, not silently converted into “Executive Committee member”. Rosental's own institutional profile explicitly lists CHEP scientific-council membership. That provides individual evidence, but cannot reconstruct the inaccessible current executive-committee page or prove the two body labels are interchangeable. [French overview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/centre-for-history-and-economics-paris-chep/); [Rosental profile, scientific responsibilities](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/annuaire/rosental-paul-andre/).

### Cambridge, Harvard and shared work

Harvard's checked organisation page names **Emma Rothschild and David Yang** under its directors heading, with **Emily Gauthier** managing administration and finance, **Jennifer Nickerson** coordinating research and fellowships, and **Amy Price** credited for websites. Its separate executive-committee page lists six people, including the two directors. Those two lists yield nine distinct people and eleven current directory assignments. They are partner records, not nine additional Paris affiliates. [Harvard Organization](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/organization.htm); [Harvard Executive Committee](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/executive.htm).

For Cambridge, an official university profile identifies **Tim Harper** as Director, corroborated by Magdalene's 2025 magazine. The centre's own pages were not retrievable in this run, so this does not establish the full current leadership. Paul Warde's directorship is documented in a 2021/22 report; other Cambridge support and associate-director titles survive in dated college publications. These are historical observations, not assumed current appointments. [Tim Harper](https://www.bennettschool.cam.ac.uk/about-us/person/tim-harper/); [Magdalene Matters 55, p. 20](https://www.magd.cam.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/Magdalene%20Matters%20Issue%2055.pdf); [CAMPOP 2021/22 report, p. 12](https://www.campop.geog.cam.ac.uk/about/annualreports/annualreport2022.pdf).

The current Harvard overview describes a Joint Center based across Harvard, Magdalene College and Sciences Po. The thematic programme **Economic History, Climate, and the Environment** explicitly names the three centres as its bases. This supports a partnership/network map and a programme connection. The 2007 Harvard announcement describes a historical joint management committee and separate executive oversight committees for Harvard and Cambridge; it cannot establish today's governance or Paris reporting relationships. [Joint Center overview](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/index.html); [EHC programme](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/); [Harvard announcement, 24 May 2007](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2007/05/harvard-cambridge-establish-joint-center-for-history-and-economics/).

### Coordination and institutional memory

Claire Lemercier's CV dates her co-coordination with David Todd to **2019–2021**. A Sciences Po interview published **21 July 2025** records Todd saying he had coordinated CHEP since **2022**. The present CHEP organisation and opened host pages name Levy, while other retrieved profile/cached versions retain Todd. We can document the succession of public descriptions, but not its effective date. A current-listing date is never substituted for an appointment date. [Lemercier CV, 12 March 2024, p. 11](https://lemercier.ouvaton.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Lemercier-CV-mars-2024.pdf); [Todd interview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/ecole-recherche/fr/actualites/entretien-avec-david-todd-directeur-des-etudes-en-histoire/).

The 2020 minutes identify ten people in a historical scientific-council discussion, with wording that distinguishes members from people the council had also included. They also suggest preparatory Paris activity more than two years earlier. This imprecise recollection is retained alongside the current 2019/2022 account; it does not justify a newly invented founding year. The fifth anniversary of establishment would be **2027**, if 2022 is the institutionally chosen milestone. [2020 minutes](https://www.sciencespo.fr/sites/default/files/cd_cp/cs/2020/2020_03_10_CSFR.pdf); [CHEP home](https://www.histecon.fr/en/).

Category counts overlap where a person holds several roles. Parallel source labels, such as research fellow/postdoctoral researcher or student/scholarship holder, are separate assertions in `roles.csv`; its row total is not an appointment count. Past fellowship and current associate status can coexist. Publication participants and selected event speakers are retained with their specific connection only: the publications page explicitly describes participants, whereas the associate directory independently establishes that category. Éric Monnet illustrates why matching names matters: an event appearance alone would be insufficient, but he also appears in the associate list. [Publications](https://histecon.fr/en/publications.html); [Associates](https://histecon.fr/en/research_associates.html).

The profile checks identify actionable corrections. CHEP still labels Alexia Yates “Manchester”, whereas EUI documents her move in September 2024. M’Hamed Oualdi’s EUI appointment coexists with a Sciences Po position in secondment from 1 September 2024; the single Sciences Po roster label misses that context. [EUI appointment notice](https://www.eui.eu/news-hub?id=four-new-professors-join-the-history-department&lang=en-GB); [Oualdi at Sciences Po](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/annuaire/oualdi-m-hamed/).

Anatole Le Bras’s past-fellows entry gives 2021–2023, but a dated Sciences Po biography says he joined CHEP in September 2023. Both assertions remain flagged; his current associate listing is separate. [Past fellows](https://histecon.fr/en/research_fellows_past.html); [Sciences Po, 23 January 2024](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/evenements/alienes-une-histoire-sociale-de-la-folie-au-xixe-siecle/).

Some institutional labels on the CHEP roster differ from newer personal profiles. These are stored as source contexts, with uncertainty recorded; a directory's listing of a person at a university is not an independently dated employment record. A scholarship cohort is likewise neither a permanent membership category nor evidence of eligibility for a future call. The four 2026 doctoral awardees are recorded as that cohort, with exact award start/end dates unknown. [Research Students 2026](https://histecon.fr/en/research_students.html); [French host community list](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/centre-for-history-and-economics-paris-chep/).

The files support three concrete student uses. **Website preparation:** filter current Paris categories and prepare short sourced profiles for review. **Annual-report attribution:** join outputs to dated roles, keeping a former fellow's later publication separate from work produced during a known appointment. **Community introductions:** search documented interests and current/historical category, then ask an academic counterpart which connections merit exploration. None requires collecting personal contact details; the profile and institutional contact URLs provide sufficient routes.

The following are **editorial connections inferred from public research descriptions**, not claims of collaboration, willingness or unmet demand. They offer a starting vocabulary for the proposed theme views.

A draft directory card should contain name, documented CHEP category, role period/precision, home institution with its own evidence, two or three research keywords, institutional profile link and last-check date. For a past fellow it should visibly say “former fellow”; for an undated listing it should say “listed on the checked directory; appointment dates to confirm”. A thematic label must remain editable: it should help a researcher discover colleagues without fixing their scholarly identity to a narrow taxonomy.

## Methods, decisions and limitations

Research followed the People navigation, fellows' and students' historical branches, English/French variants, the four starting institutions, dated institutional records, selected public profiles and targeted searches. It was bounded to Paris affiliation/governance plus partner leadership/support. It did not become a publications, grants or events census. All checked URLs and retrieval states are in [sources.csv](sources.csv), with a task-specific tree and remaining queue in [coverage.md](coverage.md).

Records separate identities from role periods and distinguish direct evidence from interpretation. Duplicate source URLs are reconciled through [source_id_crosswalk.csv](source_id_crosswalk.csv); [evidence_notes.csv](evidence_notes.csv) preserves locators and consequential caveats. Source dates, access dates and attested role periods are separate. No dated web-archive snapshot was successfully retrieved: historical claims instead use dated CVs, minutes, interviews and institutional publications. Search-engine crawl ages and copyright years are not appointment or publication dates.

Several web-tool responses are indexed copies of different ages, sometimes disagreeing for the same URL. Direct retrieval also encountered HTTP 406/403 responses. These are access limitations in this run, not proof that the public sites are broken or that language versions presently disagree. Current public wording is supported where possible by both centre and host sources, while time-sensitive uncertainties remain explicit.

The most consequential internal questions are the current Paris committee composition and remit, whether its English and French body labels refer to the same body, the coordinator transition date, how affiliation categories are assigned and ended, and who can maintain the public directory. Cambridge's full current leadership/support roster also needs verification. Staff capacity, demand for introductions, discretionary funding and reporting authority cannot be established from these web pages. The proposal therefore begins with a bounded evidence review and a reusable draft, rather than treating those unknowns as facts.
