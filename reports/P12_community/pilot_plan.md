# P12 — Twelve-week community pilot

**as_of_date: 2026-11-15. Status: proposal and reusable drafts; nothing scheduled, distributed or published.** Estimates assume five student hours a week and no confirmed discretionary budget. Week numbers begin only after an academic sponsor accepts the scope; they are not calendar bookings. Recheck term breaks, examinations and host seminar dates before assigning dates. All public evidence was accessed on 2026-11-15; source titles and retrieval limits are in `sources.csv`.

## Purpose and boundaries

Make it easier to enter an existing scholarly conversation, receive useful feedback and continue an exchange. The preferred host is the CHEP-supported [Histoires de la vie économique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/histoires-de-la-vie-economique/) reading workshop (P12-N016). Its convenors must decide whether the pilot fits; this document does not alter its programme. If an equivalent internal activity exists, add the welcome and follow-up elements there and drop the extra sessions.

Exactly three connected activities are proposed:

1. **P12-A01 — Welcome and entry guide:** one short bilingual orientation note linking authoritative programmes and stating how to request access, readings and practical information. Maintain links, rather than a second calendar or membership database.
2. **P12-A02 — Three peer sessions:** 60 minutes each in weeks 4, 7 and 10, preferably adjacent to an existing meeting but separately booked and clearly described. Use one source, a short draft and a returned draft in sequence. No invited speaker, travel or catering is required.
3. **P12-A03 — Bounded follow-up:** a voluntary exchange of one resource or one page of feedback between sessions, plus a short anonymous usefulness check. No permanent mentoring scheme or promise of unlimited matching.

The same participants may return, but each session must also work for a newcomer. Proposed size: 6–10, maximum 12; minimum four confirmed participants including a contributor before each session. These are facilitation assumptions, not evidence of demand. No participation fee; attendance confers no CHEP affiliation or funding eligibility. Allow people to join only the welcome route or one session.

## Session 1 — A source, a question, an introduction

**Week 4; 60 minutes; audience:** interested Master's and doctoral students, visitors and external researchers, subject to confirmed host admission rules. **Learning goal:** practise turning a small piece of evidence into a researchable question and make one useful scholarly connection. **Prerequisites:** none beyond reading two pages or an equivalent accessible transcription; no requirement to disclose a thesis topic.

**Preparation:** the convenor selects an openly available or author-permitted archival excerpt, table or short text related to an existing seminar theme. The student sends the link, bibliographic provenance, a plain-text version and two prompts five working days beforehand. Avoid copying a protected chapter. A participant may instead bring a one-paragraph source problem; sharing is voluntary. Estimated participant preparation: 15–20 minutes. Academic preparation: 30 minutes, within the support budget.

**Agenda:** 0–5 welcome, purpose and confidentiality; 5–12 one-sentence introductions, with the option to pass; 12–20 contributor explains source and one difficulty; 20–35 pairs identify one inference and one limit; 35–50 collective discussion of evidence, scale and alternative explanations; 50–57 each willing participant offers one method/resource or asks one question; 57–60 anonymous exit card and invitation to return.

**Support and access:** academic convenor moderates substantive claims; student facilitates time and materials. One accessible room for 12 with shared screen or printed material supplied in kind; online-only fallback on an institution-approved platform. Avoid hybrid delivery unless a separate operator is available. Announce the main spoken language; accept questions and notes in French or English, provide a short bilingual glossary, and permit written participation. Do not promise simultaneous interpretation.

**Follow-up output:** one page of methodological questions and up to five public resource links, without attributed remarks. Contributor approves any mention of their material. Optional exchange uses mutual consent before contact details are shared. Send at most one reminder through the authorised host route. No recording.

## Session 2 — A small draft, useful feedback

**Week 7; 60 minutes. Learning goal:** distinguish a claim, its evidential basis and a feasible revision. One volunteer supplies no more than two pages/800 words and a specific question; this may be a Master's proposal or doctoral/visitor research excerpt. The pilot is not a selection panel, funding assessment or thesis defence. Participant preparation: at most 30 minutes.

**Preparation:** secure the author's agreement about audience and retention. Send the excerpt through approved restricted storage five working days before; do not circulate unpublished work publicly. Offer a public-source exercise if nobody wants to share a draft. Student prepares the three-field feedback card: “claim I understood”; “evidence/question to clarify”; “one possible next step”. Two volunteers may open discussion, but nobody is named in advance without agreement.

**Agenda:** 0–5 newcomer orientation and no-recording reminder; 5–10 author frames the request; 10–20 quiet reading and paired feedback; 20–35 two short responses plus author clarification; 35–45 group discusses alternative explanations and practical source/method options; 45–50 pairs exchange one question from their own research and one possible next step, with an option to pass; 50–57 author chooses up to two next steps; 57–60 anonymous usefulness check. Convenor prevents status-based pile-ons and makes room for written contributions.

**Room/platform/language:** same conditions as session 1. Drafts may be in French or English; confirm the group can read the chosen language before circulation. Provide an author-approved short summary if useful; do not require the student to translate a full draft.

**Follow-up output:** private author-owned feedback card and, if permission is given, an anonymised generic methods tip for the resource note. Offer one voluntary 15-minute peer exchange within two weeks. Do not track whether named individuals complied. Delete shared draft copies after the pilot, or earlier at the author's request, subject to the host's approved storage rules.

## Session 3 — What changed, and where to go next?

**Week 10; 60 minutes. Learning goal:** test whether an earlier exchange changed a research decision and identify one continued conversation worth pursuing. Newcomers are welcome: provide a one-page recap that contains no confidential draft material. Preparation: up to 20 minutes; author optionally returns with one revised paragraph/table. If no author returns, use a public-source revision exercise instead and record that sustained follow-up was not established.

**Agenda:** 0–8 orientation and recap; 8–18 contributor describes a revision or unresolved obstacle; 18–33 small groups compare the old/new question using authorised material; 33–45 method/resource exchange in which participants may ask their own source question and receive a concrete suggestion, including an optional visitor contribution if someone has volunteered; 45–53 participants identify a specific next research conversation or choose none; 53–60 anonymous continuation feedback and closure. No assumption that any named CHEP scholar will attend.

**Support/access:** academic convenor selects the manageable question and attends; student uses the same room/platform and language arrangements. External participation requires the host's practical access confirmation; no international travel is built into this session.

**Follow-up output:** a one-page resource sheet with public URLs and an aggregate note on whether feedback was used, plus the final evaluation in week 12. Keep open research questions separate from claims of successful partnership. No new mailing list is created.

## Gates and handover

Use the exact thresholds and missing-data rules in [needs_assessment.md](needs_assessment.md). Do not start without an academic sponsor, confirmed access route and minimum interest; do not treat survey silence as approval. At week 6, review burden and evidence of usefulness. After week 12, no automatic renewal: continue only if support, demand and manageable effort are all present. If sessions stop, retain a checked welcome note only when an institutional owner accepts its upkeep; otherwise hand over the draft and archive the pilot.
