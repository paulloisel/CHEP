# P12 — An easier way into CHEP’s research conversations

**as_of_date: 2026-11-15. Research and proposals only.** P12 alone was executed, using the entire shared brief and `Summary.md`. Other workstreams were neither required nor used. All public sources were accessed on 15 November 2026; full titles, dates and retrieval limitations are recorded in [sources.csv](sources.csv). No invitations, messages, subscriptions, applications, website changes or publication were made.

## Decision brief — one page

**What we learned.** There is a strong existing base for a modest community programme. The CHEP-supported [Histoires de la vie économique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/histoires-de-la-vie-economique/) workshop has a 2026–27 programme and explicitly welcomes students and researchers from Sciences Po and elsewhere (P12-N016). Other provision includes a [joint online seminar](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/seminars/index.html), dated visitor entries and reported Cambridge–Paris workshops. These establish opportunities, not unmet demand. Current entry information is sometimes available on host pages while retrieved CHEP aliases and archives show older states. This is a reason to link to authoritative event information, not proof that the live website is broken.

**What CHEP can do.** Test a twelve-week programme that helps newcomers enter existing activities and gives willing participants a small, repeatable feedback exchange. Use one academic sponsor and the existing seminar infrastructure. The three actions form one pilot; they are not three new series.

| Recommended action | Finding → benefit | Student effort, estimated | Support, cash assumption and success measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A01: one welcome and entry guide** | Existing access routes are spread across host and centre pages → help newcomers find an appropriate next step | 6 setup h + 6 maintenance h over 12 weeks | Institutional access/editorial counterpart; €0 if existing channel available; a newcomer can identify event, access route and preparation without unresolved essential information |
| **A02: three 60-minute peer sessions** | Existing discussion provision plus documented comparator feedback practices → test useful discussion of a small source or draft | 5 setup h + 21 preparation/delivery/follow-up h | Academic convenor and room/platform; €0 conditional baseline; at least two respondents receive actionable feedback in each final session |
| **A03: bounded optional follow-up** | Existing cross-site connections provide a basis for research exchange → test whether conversations continue | 3 setup h + 11 recurring/evaluation h | Mutual consent and approved institutional channel; €0; anonymous returning-participant and continued-conversation counts, with denominators |

Total: **14 setup + 38 recurring = 52 student hours**, averaging **4.33 hours/week**, with **8 contingency hours** within a 60-hour ceiling. Separate estimated support is **8 academic hours and 3 administrative/editor hours**. No discretionary budget is confirmed; the €0 baseline depends on in-kind infrastructure. Optional refreshments would add €36, only if approved. Named scholars have made no commitments.

**What the student can deliver next.** Take the completed welcome-page draft, needs instrument and three session plans to the Coordinator and an appropriate convenor. In the first two pilot weeks, resolve ownership, access and overlap with existing informal activity; week three is the interest test. If current provision already meets the need, retain only the guide or integrate a small introduction into that provision.

**Decision needed.** Identify an academic sponsor, decide whether the peer format belongs within an existing group, and confirm the support/access route. Do not approve a standing series on the strength of public research alone. Continue after week twelve only with manageable effort, available support and the modest participation/usefulness evidence defined below.

## Findings and implications

### Start with access to existing scholarly life

CHEP's [organisation page](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html) calls Jonathan Levy **Coordinator**, not head or director (P12-S003). The host [CHEP overview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/research/centre-for-history-and-economics-in-paris-chep/) places it at CHSP and distinguishes the collaboration beginning in 2019 from establishment in 2022 (P12-N001). Neither institutional association nor event attendance implies a reporting relationship, formal membership or eligibility for every partner programme.

This distinction matters most for newcomers. The English [student page](https://histecon.fr/en/research_students.html) is a dated roster, while the French [Participants](https://histecon.fr/fr/participants.html) navigation calls its corresponding branch *Doctorant·e·s* (P12-S005; P12-S017). A Master's student should therefore enter through an expressly accessible activity rather than assume the directory is an application portal. The proposed welcome guide separates participation, affiliation and funding, with a clear route for each question.

The programme should complement existing training. [Le Colloque junior](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/les-formations/les-etudes-doctorales/le-colloque-junior/) already offers an eligibility-dependent doctoral conference-support route (P12-N008). Recommending another student conference would create more work before establishing a missing benefit. Similarly, the [2027 CHEP grant call](https://histecon.fr/en/research_grants.html) covers specified early-stage Sciences Po doctoral students, not Master's students (P12-S009). A welcome service can point people to the responsible scheme without becoming a separate funding advisory programme.

### Distinguish current programmes from historical precedents

The [Climate and Capitalism reading schedule](https://histecon.fr/climate_capitalism/readings.html) documents meetings in late 2024 and an undated spring 2025 continuation (P12-S007). Its present operation could not be established. It is valuable evidence that a related reading format has existed, and a question for the convenor, rather than a blank space to fill with a duplicate group.

The regional [L’histoire économique en région parisienne](https://histecon.fr/fr/agenda.html) guide likewise documents a useful visitor-oriented discovery service, but the returned list ends in June 2025 (P12-S008). Current maintenance cannot be inferred from its stated monthly updating practice. The proposed guide therefore points to host calendars and carries a review date instead of copying a large regional schedule.

For international connections, the [Paris events retrospective](https://histecon.fr/en/events_recent.html) reports a second Cambridge–Paris workshop on 26 June 2026, superseding an older partner archive's future-tense reference to summer 2026 (P12-N021; P12-N013). This establishes a documented continuation of exchange. It does not establish demand for another cross-border meeting, open admission, available travel money or anyone's willingness to join the pilot. A local resource exchange or existing online route is the proportionate first step.

### Entry rules differ by audience and activity

Four journeys are developed in [community_map.md](community_map.md): a new Master's student, a doctoral affiliate, a visitor and a Paris-based external collaborator. Each distinguishes a documented route from a proposed improvement. The intended benefit is practical: know what to read, how to request access, whether listening is acceptable and where to take a small research question.

Access cannot be generalised across programmes. [La matière du politique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/la-matiere-du-politique/) requires registration and distinguishes an internal activity; the current page does not establish the hybrid modality described in an older alias (P12-N012; P12-N006). Formal [CHSP research stays](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/sejours-de-recherche/) have their own eligibility and sponsorship conditions (P12-N015). The welcome prototype links to these responsible sources, avoiding a promise that every newcomer may simply attend or use campus facilities.

## What the comparators contribute

Five selected centres/networks supply concrete mechanisms, recorded in [comparator_practices.csv](comparator_practices.csv). [IHR History Lab](https://www.history.ac.uk/about-us/history-lab) demonstrates explicit postgraduate entry rules and brief research introductions (P12-C001). Historical [Economic History Society workshops](https://ehs.org.uk/event/third-online-phd-thesis-workshop/) allocate a distinct role to feedback (P12-C002; P12-C003). [CERI's doctoral China workshop](https://www.sciencespo.fr/ceri/fr/content/atelier-doctoral-sur-la-chine-contemporaine) makes unfinished research discussable through circulated working material (P12-C004). The [EUI Intellectual History Working Group](https://www.eui.eu/research-hub?id=intellectual-history) combines researcher coordination with academic backing (P12-C005). Cambridge's [AHSS bulletin on CRASSH Connections](https://www.ahssresearch.group.cam.ac.uk/ahss-research-and-impact-bulletin) makes logistical support and a funding ceiling visible (P12-C006).

The useful transfers are small: explain entry, limit preparation, allocate feedback time, retain an academic backstop and count administrative work. The comparison spans different documented operating arrangements; actual budgets and paid staffing are unknown for most examples. It supplies no effectiveness ranking. The CRASSH ceiling is not a CHEP budget, the EHS examples are historical, and comparator admission rules cannot be imported into Paris. The practice-transfer matrix ties each idea to a specific need hypothesis and existing CHEP provision, with evidence still required before adoption.

## The proposed pilot in practice

The preferred pilot is **one welcome guide, three peer sessions and bounded follow-up**, described fully in [pilot_plan.md](pilot_plan.md). The sessions progress from a source question to a short draft and then a revision or unresolved research obstacle. Returning people can develop continuity; a short recap allows newcomers to join later. None requires an eminent speaker or a polished paper. An author can choose a public-source exercise instead of exposing unpublished work.

Materials are deliberately small: a short source, a maximum two-page draft, or a revised paragraph/table. Participants receive one focused question before the meeting. Each session ends with a concrete output: a resource note, private feedback card or next-step question. These are tools for scholarly help, not publication targets. No output is attributed or publicly circulated without the contributor's agreement.

Language access is part of the design rather than an unspecified translation task. Announce the main spoken language, accept French or English questions where the convenor can accommodate them, provide short bilingual logistical text and allow written contributions. A room with accessible materials or an approved online-only session is feasible in principle. Hybrid delivery needs a separate operator; it should not silently become the student's responsibility. The academic host selects suitable material and moderates substantive feedback.

The research-theme suggestions in the map connect public work on land and compensation, state/investment, and materials/environmental history. They are editorial possibilities, not proposed appointments. Nobody should be recruited merely because a profile contains a matching keyword. A source question offered voluntarily by an early-career participant is sufficient to launch the format.

The 52-hour plan includes setup, delivery, maintenance and evaluation. It leaves genuine spare capacity in lighter weeks, while every scheduled week stays at or below five hours. The student's attendance at unrelated seminars, full-text translation, new software, permanent mentoring and international event production are outside scope. If the host cannot supply the required support, narrow the offer to a reviewed entry guide. The no-cash scenario is viable only if those in-kind inputs exist.

## Learning, stopping and continuation

The six neutral questions in [needs_assessment.md](needs_assessment.md) ask what already works, what help would be useful, and which arrangements fit. They explicitly allow “none” and “unsure”. The instrument has not been distributed. Up to three short voluntary conversations can clarify weak responses without turning a small trial into a survey project.

Week two checks sponsorship and practical support; week three checks interest. Before a session, require four confirmed participants including one willing contributor. After session one, low attendance or insufficient useful-feedback evidence triggers one adjustment. If the next session still lacks the defined minimum, stop further sessions. These are proposed management thresholds, not validated research benchmarks.

At week twelve, recommend continuation only if support remains, average student effort is within five hours, and each of the last two sessions has at least two returning answers and two respondents reporting actionable feedback received. Keep denominators visible. Missing feedback is insufficient evidence, not zero benefit or success. Anonymous self-reports about continued conversations strengthen the case without being equated with collaborations. Do not create persistent identifiers, named engagement scores or unique-person totals from repeat headcounts.

## Methods, decisions and limitations

The focused corpus covers Paris community routes and current advertised provision, with relevant historical evidence from 2019 to the cutoff, dated visitor appointments extending into 2027, and advertised 2026–27 programmes. It selectively follows CHSP, Cambridge and Harvard connections plus five comparators. It is not an exhaustive people, event, publication or funding census. [coverage.md](coverage.md) gives the source tree, counts, exclusions, discrepancies and remaining queue.

Research followed official navigation and targeted searches, opening underlying sources where possible. Returned web representations expose differing crawl ages; live-site freshness was not independently verified. Failed retrievals are recorded as limits, not broken-link diagnoses. Search snippets were treated as leads, and current host pages took precedence over older aliases. No archive-snapshot dates were supplied. Public pages cannot establish private provision, demand, satisfaction, staff capacity, room availability or participant willingness. Those are the specific questions the proposed pilot must resolve before it becomes an ongoing commitment.
