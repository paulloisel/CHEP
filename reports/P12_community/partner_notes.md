## Findings for the programme design

**The strongest anchor is an existing, explicitly accessible workshop.** [Histoires de la vie économique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/histoires-de-la-vie-economique/) (P12-N016, verified) is a monthly collective reading workshop, supported by CHEP and proposed by Nicolas Delalande and David Todd. The page explicitly welcomes students, doctoral researchers and academics from Sciences Po and elsewhere. Participants read approximately fifty pages; one or more session leaders introduce collective discussion. The 2026–27 calendar is present. This supports improving newcomer orientation and reading access before adding a competing reading series. Actual room admission, registration and lawful access to readings still require event-level checking.

The next listed session, [Le retour des inégalités ? Bilan et perspectives historiographiques](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/evenements/le-retour-des-inegalites-bilan-et-perspectives-historiographiques/) (P12-N018), is **announced** for 17 November 2026, 12:30–14:00 at 1 place Saint-Thomas-d’Aquin. Its event page displays registration and reduced-mobility accessibility information. This is an example of an actionable entry point, not a proposed pilot launch date or confirmation the session occurred.

[La matière du politique](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/la-matiere-du-politique/) (P12-N012, verified) has a 2026–27 programme on *Matières et matériaux* and requires registration. Its calendar includes a reading introduction and an expressly internal study day. The current practical section does **not** promise hybrid access. The older [.html route](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/content/la-matiere-du-politique.html) (P12-N006) was retrieved from a tool-reported 1.7-year-old crawl and contains 2024–25 hybrid wording. Do not carry that historical modality into a current welcome page. Its current coordination list includes Rébecca Mathiot; this documents a research connection, not willingness to join the pilot.

[Joint Center for History and Economics Online Seminar](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/seminars/index.html) (P12-N017, verified) explicitly connects Cambridge, Harvard, Paris and other friends/affiliates. The retrieved Fall 2026 page offers Harvard in-person and live Zoom participation, approximately twenty minutes of presentation followed by thirty minutes of discussion. It lists 28 October 2026; no subsequent dated session was established here. Recommend a link to the existing series and recheck registration before suggesting attendance, rather than creating a separate international seminar. The search result had said the 2026–27 programme was forthcoming; the opened page superseded that stale indexed wording.

[Economic History, Climate, and the Environment](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/) (P12-N002, verified) states that this initiative is based at Cambridge CHE, CHE-P and Harvard CHE. It links Cambridge’s environmental reading group and the Sciences Po seminar. This is a documented thematic bridge; individual project locations and access rules remain distinct. The [reading archive](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/reading_group.html) (P12-N007) describes weekly Cambridge meetings and participant-recommended texts, with 2026–27 entries. Paris or remote admission is **unverified**. A reading-list pointer is safe; promising a Paris place is not.

The [EHCE archive](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/activities_recent.html) (P12-N013) retrospectively records a Cambridge–Paris workshop on 26 June 2025 and the Paris *Climate and Capitalism* reading workshop during 2024–25. It still describes a summer 2026 follow-up as future. The more recent Paris [recent events page](https://histecon.fr/en/events_recent.html) (P12-N021) reports a second workshop on **26 June 2026**, convened by Giacomo Parrinello, Pedro Ramos Pinto and Paul Warde, at which participants presented research and discussed future collaborations. Thus a 2026 continuation is documented; the older forward-looking sentence is superseded. The linked Cambridge programme could not be retrieved. Proposed introductions can build on this existing exchange, without claiming any participant has committed to new activity.

## Host and access boundaries

The [Sciences Po CHEP overview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/en/research/centre-for-history-and-economics-in-paris-chep/) (P12-N001) situates CHEP at CHSP, distinguishes collaboration in 2019 from establishment in 2022, and calls Jonathan Levy **CHEP coordinator**. The listed community is selective: it cannot establish that unlisted students are excluded.

[Le Colloque junior](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/les-formations/les-etudes-doctorales/le-colloque-junior/) (P12-N008, verified) already provides a route for doctoral conference organisation with CHSP and École de la recherche support. Its standing rules require at least two CHSP doctoral organisers, not beyond their fourth doctoral year, a scientific case, indicative programme, scientific committee and budget. No current competition deadline was established. This is a referral for eligible doctoral affiliates, not a Master's entitlement or a reason for this student to organise another conference.

The [Master d’histoire page](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/les-formations/master-histoire/) (P12-N010, verified) describes methodological training and dissertation research within the degree. It supplies a degree-based route to advice; it does not grant CHEP membership. A CHEP pilot should complement that teaching rather than promise supervision or formal methods training.

[CHSP Séjours de recherche](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/sejours-de-recherche/) (P12-N015) documents a formal application route: doctoral enrolment of at least a year, full funding and permanent-faculty sponsorship under its junior-researcher eligibility section; normally one to five months. Listed benefits include campus/library access and shared office space subject to availability. The page also has a professor route and mentions exchange agreements. This is CHSP provision, not automatic CHEP visitor status. Its heading includes postdoctoral researchers while the stated doctoral-enrolment criterion needs clarification for that group. The specific spring 2027 deadline, 2 November 2026, has passed even though the page says applications are currently open; do not advertise it as open. The dates and ambiguous wording need administrative confirmation before any referral/application advice.

[Harvard Tea Talks](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/tea_talks.html) (P12-N011, verified) describe one-hour informal meetings with presentations capped at twenty minutes, roundtable discussion, a Harvard room and refreshments; Fall 2026 dates are listed. This is a usable format precedent, not evidence of a Paris service or of success. [Harvard’s home](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/) (P12-N004) describes student exchanges with Cambridge and a public Bluesky news route, but does not establish entitlement for all Paris students.
