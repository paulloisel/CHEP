## Corpus and counting rules

The question is what modest community programme can build on documented Paris provision. Included: participation and communication routes; students, fellows, associates and visitor categories where relevant to access or research connections; current advertised host/partner programmes; selected historical precedents from 2019 to 15 November 2026; future academic-year programme entries and dated appointments where explicitly advertised. Institution-wide membership, exhaustive publication/funding histories and every historical event are excluded. Cambridge and Harvard activities are kept separate unless a Paris connection is explicit.

`sources.csv` contains **72 records for 72 distinct URLs**: **40 substantively retrieved official-page representations**, **1 retrieved site 404**, and **31 unresolved retrievals/search-only leads**. The denominator is the attempted, registered source corpus, not all pages on these sites. URL aliases are retained separately to preserve retrieval differences; no event or person totals are computed from them. The source register includes 42 main Paris/discovery records, 22 host/partner records and eight comparator records. `partner_sources.csv` and `comparator_sources.csv` are component registers copied into the consolidated file, not extra sources to add to its count.

The analytical deliverables contain **14 provision records, four audience journeys, three theme connections, five transfer rows, five comparator institutions/networks, three proposed activities, three session plans and six needs-assessment questions**. The report has a one-page decision brief before its analysis. The 12-week resource plan is 14 setup + 38 recurring = 52 student hours; eight spare hours remain within the 60-hour scenario.

No coverage percentage is claimed. No participant population denominator, attendance series, satisfaction data, private group inventory or actual spending total was available. Unknown is not zero.
