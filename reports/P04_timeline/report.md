# P04 — CHEP institutional history and public programme

**As of 15 November 2026. Research and planning draft; nothing has been published or sent.**

## Decision brief

**What we learned.** CHEP has a layered history. Current institutional accounts date the collaboration to **2019** and formal establishment to **2022**. A historical CHEP report calls the workshop of **5 June 2019** a launch event. Earlier Sciences Po minutes complicate the prehistory: in March 2020, the Paris extension was described as already more than two years old. These are different claims, with different precision, rather than a licence to choose a single convenient founding date. A **2027 fifth anniversary of formal establishment** is defensible, provided the institution confirms that framing and any specific anniversary day. [CHEP history](https://histecon.fr/fr/); [2019 workshop report](https://histecon.fr/en/spatial.html); [2020 council minutes, pp.14–15](https://www.sciencespo.fr/sites/default/files/cd_cp/cs/2020/2020_03_10_CSFR.pdf); [dated 2026 institutional account](https://www.sciencespo.fr/ecole-recherche/fr/actualites/quatre-jeunes-chercheurs-histoire-laureats-bourse-chep-2026/).

The accompanying register contains **148 census records plus three unverified leads**. The census includes **12 future announcements** and **32 records documented as held**. These are evidence counts within the checked corpus, not CHEP output or attendance totals. Centre-supported activities, a linked Sciences Po series, and the shared online programme are distinguished. The programme already offers sustained discussion of economic life, environmental history, and social and legal questions; the immediate opportunity is to make that history reliable and reusable.

**What CHEP can do with it.** Use the chronology for an anniversary introduction and filter the event register for annual reporting. Preserve the distinction between Paris responsibility and wider-network provision. The dedicated [organisation page](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html) calls Jonathan Levy **Coordinator**; it does not provide an appointment date.

**What the student can deliver next.** Three proposed actions fit the planning scenario of five student hours per week and no confirmed discretionary budget. ## Origin-date assessment

The 2019/2022 distinction survives comparison of the current French and English CHEP accounts and the dated January 2026 award announcement. The 2022 Sciences Po research report also describes CHEP being welcomed within the Centre d’histoire, with David Todd leading it. This is stronger support than a website copyright year. It does not identify a constitutive act or exact establishment day. [Research at Sciences Po 2022, printed p.129](https://www.sciencespo.fr/cevipof/sites/sciencespo.fr.cevipof/files/LaRechercheaSciencesPo-2022.pdf).

The spatial-history workshop is the earliest precisely dated CHEP event located. Its report says it took place at CSO, Sciences Po, on 5 June 2019, and identifies it as a launch. The fifth anniversary of that event therefore fell on 5 June 2024. A 2027 celebration should acknowledge this earlier activity and identify its basis as establishment, rather than imply that no centre or community existed before 2022. [Workshop account](https://histecon.fr/en/spatial.html).

The March 2020 minutes suggest an earlier Paris phase, before roughly March 2018. They do not identify its start or explain its relationship to the official 2019 chronology. The inventory preserves this uncertainty and does not manufacture a 2018 anniversary.

Other pre-establishment evidence supports continuity. The host history records students selected for exchanges in 2020 and informal discussions during interrupted travel, followed by Lola Zappi’s Harvard stay in 2021–22. CHEP’s former-fellows page places Elsa Génard’s fellowship in 2021–22. A council record of October 2021 already discusses CHEP as a Sciences Po–Harvard–Cambridge undertaking. These show activity before the later institutional milestone, without proving that all planned travel happened. [Host history](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/centre-for-history-and-economics-paris-chep/); [past fellows](https://histecon.fr/en/research_fellows_past.html); [2021 minutes, printed p.10](https://www.sciencespo.fr/sites/default/files/cd_cp/cs/2021/2021_10_12_CSFR.pdf).

Leadership should also be presented as dated evidence. Todd described coordinating CHEP since 2022 in a July 2025 interview. Dedicated CHEP and host pages now name Levy, while Todd’s profile and the July 2026 State and Capital programme retain coordinator wording. The historical record does not justify an exact handover date or an uninterrupted sequence of single-person leadership. [Todd interview](https://www.sciencespo.fr/ecole-recherche/fr/actualites/entretien-avec-david-todd-directeur-des-etudes-en-histoire/); [Todd profile](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/chercheur/David%20Todd/86024.html); [July programme](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/evenements/state-and-capital-old-problems-new-trajectories/); [current organisation](https://histecon.fr/en/organisation.html).

Recommended public framing, pending institutional review: **“Five years since CHEP’s formal establishment in 2022, with a longer history of Paris collaboration and a public launch workshop in 2019.”** A retrospective prepared now should use 15 November 2026 as its evidence cutoff. It can retain a distinct prehistory section and add later material before a 2027 edition; it should not present future announcements as completed achievements.

## Programming patterns and their implications

**Recurring discussion is already an institutional strength visible in the programme.** The economic-life seminar was announced as new in September 2022 and uses collective readings. Its themes include sanctions, empire, labour, law, gender, finance and environmental change. This provides an existing route for student participation; the calendar alone cannot establish how welcoming or well-attended it is. A student contribution could make the readings, access conditions and subsequent discussion records easier to locate, subject to convenor agreement. [Dated introduction](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/actualites/nouveaux-seminaires/); [current format and calendars](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/histoires-de-la-vie-economique/).

**Environmental history links formats and institutions.** The 2022 roundtable, the 2024 emotions/climate workshop and the 2025 Paris–Cambridge workshop create a documented sequence of exchanges. The linked material-politics series moves through war, oceans and materials across successive academic years. These are editorial connections between public programmes, not proof of causal influence or a common management structure. CHEP is explicitly one of the bases of Economic History, Climate, and the Environment. [CHEP retrospective events](https://histecon.fr/en/events_recent.html); [joint programme](https://histecon.fas.harvard.edu/ehc/); [historical seminar calendars](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/seminaires/seminaire-des-axes-et-des-domaines/la-matiere-du-politique/).

**Junior and social-history activity belongs in the institutional story.** The host history records L’atelier du CHEP in 2023–24; the junior-colloquium archive identifies CHEP-supported meetings on empire and displacement. Separate programmes establish support for international-law and filiation conferences. These formats broaden the evidence beyond senior lectures and books. The sensible first step is to recover the programme and outputs of existing activities. The record cannot establish participation, feedback quality, or whether an informal format continues today. [Host history](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/recherche/centre-for-history-and-economics-paris-chep/); [junior archive](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/les-formations/les-etudes-doctorales/le-colloque-junior/); [international-law programme](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/sites/sciencespo.fr.histoire/files/2025-11-27ET28-INVISIBLE-ACTORS-VERSION5.pdf); [filiation conference](https://www.sciencespo.fr/histoire/fr/evenements/colloque-junior-quand-la-filiation-ne-va-pas-de-soi-filiations-et-violences-x-xe-xx-ie-siecles/).

**The main observable gap is evidence continuity.** Some pages use retrospective language; others merely preserve an announcement. A recording or explicit statement that a seminar occurred supports “documented as held”; a programme, registration link or elapsed date does not. This matters for annual reporting: a count of notices answers a different question from a count of delivered events. None of these records supports attendance totals, the composition of audiences, expenditure, or an assessment of scholarly impact.

The register also reveals correctable discrepancies. The Belonging workshop has conflicting year labels, and the Invisible Actors conference has conflicting day ranges. The economic-life calendar explicitly postpones the furniture session without a replacement date, and revises Claire-Lise Gaillard’s session from 11 to 25 March 2025. The material-politics calendar contains “2924” in its 2023–24 section; normalization to 2024 is labelled inferred. An archive workflow should preserve original notices, add corrections and state which evidence supports the selected date. Detailed evidence and unresolved variants remain in the CSV notes.

## Methods, decisions and limitations

Only P04 was executed. Summary.md supplied the four institutional starting points and no additional substantive brief. No other P01–P12 report was required. Sources were mapped before extraction, then compared across language versions, aliases, host and partner sites. Titles, dates, named participants and institutional credits were extracted as factual metadata; topic labels are descriptive rather than claims about a speaker’s argument. Public contact details were not harvested.

Web retrieval often returned indexed copies with different crawl ages. One French home route returned an old 2024-facing copy, while another returned newer content. Search extracts occasionally disagreed with opened pages. These differences are logged as retrieval limitations, not proof that the live website is broken or that a current appointment has ended. Institutional PDFs were inspected in text; failed screenshot requests limit visual verification, not the quoted page locators. Robots and sitemap retrieval failed, and no bulk automated crawl was undertaken.

This is a broad, reproducible inventory within the checked public corpus, **not a claim of complete institutional history**. Remaining gaps include early agreements, the exact establishment and handover dates, inaccessible historical Cambridge pages, previous student cohorts, missing programme files, and evidence of delivery for many sessions. Coverage details and retrieval queues are supplied separately. Internal records are needed to settle these points and to establish costs, participation or demand. The proposed maintenance work converts those limits into specific, reviewable questions.
